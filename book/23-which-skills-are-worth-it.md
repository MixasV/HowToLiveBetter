### 1. If you are under 16 years old, there is no option to "go work": the company that recruits you will be fined 5,000 yuan per month, and all those willing to recruit you are illegal workers.

- Say it in plain language: Junior high school graduates are usually 15 to 16 years old, so they are stuck on this line.The Compulsory Education Law stipulates that parents should send their children to school to accept and complete compulsory education, and employers are prohibited from recruiting children who should still be in school. ### 2. Put "Is there any use in studying" into the mortality account: for each additional year of education, the risk of adult death decreases by about 1.9%
<!-- Cost tag: money=0 time=many perseverance=yes profit=big caliber=mortality rate -->
- Cost: The time and energy spent studying for more years. For tuition fees, see Article 4 of this section.
- Say it in plain language: The results compiled from 59 countries, 603 studies, and more than 10,000 observation points are: there is a dose relationship between years of education and mortality. For every additional year of schooling, the risk of death in adulthood is reduced by about 1.9% on average. The most obvious one is between the ages of 18 and 49, where the decrease is about 2.9% for each additional year, and it drops to 0.8% for those over 70 years old.No difference is seen between men and women, or between poor and rich countries.In other words, the difference between going to high school and only going to junior high school is not just the salary.
- Benefit: Systematic review and meta-analysis, searched from January 1980 to May 2023, and finally included 603 studies, 70 sites in 59 countries, and 10,355 observations: Education has a dose-response relationship with all-cause adult mortality. For each additional year of education, the risk of death decreases by an average of 1.9% (95% uncertainty interval 1.8–2.0); for people aged 18 to 49 years, the risk decreases for each additional year.2.9% (2.8–3.0) and 0.8% (0.6–1.0) for those over 70 years old; no differences in effects stratified by sex or sociodemographic index were found
- Evidence level: A (disputed)
- Source: IHME-CHAIN Collaborators (2024). Effects of education on adult mortality: a global systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Public Health, 9(3), e155-e165. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00306-7>
- Note: Controversy.The author himself reported publication bias (p<0.0001) and high heterogeneity among studies; all included were observational studies, and years of education are highly intertwined with family conditions, region, and occupation. How much of this 1.9% is caused by education itself, and how much is due to "people who can continue studying are inherently better off." This type of research cannot be used to separate.Therefore, it is strong evidence that "reading more books and living longer are in the same direction", but not a causal guarantee that "reading books will extend your life by 1.9%".The beneficiary is yourself

### 10. When choosing skills, give priority to "whether you need to do it, whether you need to judge on the spot". This type is the most difficult to be eliminated by automation.
<!-- Cost tag: Money=0 Time=little Perseverance=No Benefit=big Caliber=money -->
- Cost: $0; go through this dimension before choosing
- Say it in plain language: In an assessment of 702 occupations in the United States, about 47% of the positions fall in the high-risk range. What blocks the machine are three things: manual dexterity, creativity, and the ability to deal with people.Therefore, jobs that require on-site work, non-standard environment, and on-the-spot judgment are more difficult to be dismissed. Positions with fixed processes and mainly processing information are at the highest risk.This study rated occupations as a whole, and later estimates broken down by specific tasks were much lower. Don't focus on the 47% number, look at those three dimensions.
- Benefit: Calculable probability assessment of 702 occupations in the United States: approximately 47% of jobs are in the high-risk range.In the model, three types of "engineering bottlenecks" determine the level of risk - perception and operation (finger dexterity, hand dexterity, working in narrow spaces and awkward postures), creative intelligence, and social intelligence.In other words, jobs with non-standard environments that require manual effort and on-the-spot judgment have a systematically lower probability of being replaced; while jobs with fixed processes and focused on information processing have the highest risk.
- Evidence level: B

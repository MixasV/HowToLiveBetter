# 29. After a big blow

The death of a loved one, being informed of a serious illness, unemployment, divorce - these things have one thing in common in the study: not only the mood but also the body are affected, and the peak incidence period is concentrated in the first few weeks to the first year.This section does not talk about how to "come out". It only talks about which things must be monitored during this period, which decisions must be postponed, and when to register.The first nine calibers are mortality, and the last two are money.

See Section 25 for the procedures at the time of the death of a loved one, the body and the money that should be withdrawn. See Section 17 for the guardianship and will of the elderly during his lifetime. See Section 7 for what he can receive when he is unemployed. See Sections 3 and 22 for exercise and light when he is depressed. When he has suicidal thoughts, call 12356 first. See Section 1, Article 25. This section will not be repeated.

### 1. Don’t be alone in the first few days after your loved one passes away. If you have heart disease, high blood pressure, or diabetes, take medicine as usual. If you feel pain in your chest, call 120
<!-- Cost tag: Money = 0 Time = Less Perseverance = No Benefit = Large Caliber = Mortality -->
- Cost: 0, just don’t be alone and don’t stop taking medication
- Say it in plain language: Within 24 hours after the death of a loved one, the probability of having a myocardial infarction is 21.1 times higher than usual, and then decreases day by day.The rate of a heart attack or stroke within 30 days of a partner's death among people aged 60 to 89 was 0.16 percent, compared with 0.08 percent among peers who were not widowed, roughly doubling before falling back a month later.The absolute numbers are not big: for those who originally had a 5% risk of myocardial infarction in ten years, there would be about one more case for every 1,394 bereaved people; for those who had the original risk of 20%, there would be one more case for every 320 people - so this is mainly for people who already have cardiovascular disease.Don't stop taking the medicine in the past few days. If you feel chest pain, shortness of breath, or weakness in one arm or leg, call 120 immediately.
- Benefit: Case-crossover analysis of 1985 patients with acute myocardial infarction: 270 people (13.6%) had lost a significant other within 6 months before the onset, 19 of them within 1 day before the myocardial infarction; the rate of acute myocardial infarction increased 21.1 times (95% CI 13.1–34.1) within 24 hours after the death of the important person, and then decreased day by day; the absolute risk was 5% risk of myocardial infarction per person over ten years1,394 people exposed, 1 more case, 20%, 1 more case for every 320 people exposed.30,447 widowed persons aged 60 to 89 years in the UK and 83,588 matched controls: 50 (0.16%) vs 67 (0.08%) persons with MI or stroke within 30 days of widowhood, IRR 2.20 (1.52–3.15), MI 2.14 (1.20–3.81), stroke2.40 (1.22–4.71), weakening after 30 days; non-MI acute coronary syndrome IRR within 90 days 2.20 (1.12–4.29), pulmonary embolism 2.37 (1.18–4.75)
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Mostofsky E, et al. (2012). Risk of acute myocardial infarction after the death of a significant person in one's life: the Determinants of Myocardial Infarction Onset Study. ### 2. The week after you are informed of the diagnosis of a serious illness, don’t go get the report alone. Decisions other than treatment should be postponed until later.
<!-- Cost tag: Money = 0 Time = Less Perseverance = No Benefit = Large Caliber = Mortality -->
- Cost: 0, find someone to accompany you and postpone the big decision for a few days
- Say it in plain language: In the first week after receiving a cancer diagnosis, the probability of suicide is 12.6 times that of people without a diagnosis, and the probability of dying from cardiovascular disease is 5.6 times; by the first year, the probability drops to 3.1 times and (in the first four weeks) 3.3 times respectively. The worse the prognosis of the cancer, the more obvious it is.This is not a problem of "psychological quality", but a common reaction of people in the days after receiving bad news.So: Take someone with you on the day you get the report. Don’t be alone when you go home. If you have chest pain or can’t breathe, call 120 immediately. Leave the treatment plan, whether you should resign, or whether you should sell the house until the next outpatient visit.
- Benefit: Historical cohort of 6,073,240 people in Sweden from 1991 to 2006: relative risk of suicide in the first week after diagnosis 12.6 (95% CI 8.6–17.8, incidence rate 2.50/1000 person-years) and in the first year 3.1 (2.7–3.5, 0.60/1000 person-years) compared with those without a cancer diagnosisperson-years); cardiovascular death 5.6 (5.2–5.9, 116.80/1000 person-years) in the first week and 3.3 (3.1–3.4, 65.81/1000 person-years) in the first 4 weeks; the increased risk fell rapidly within the first year after diagnosis, with the most obvious increase in cancer types with poor prognosis; self-control analysis of the same group of patients concluded consistent
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Fang F, et al. (2012). Suicide and cardiovascular death after a cancer diagnosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1110307>
- Note: This is cancer data. Extrapolation to other major diagnoses does not have the same strength as the original text.The absolute number is very small (suicide rate in the first week is 2.50/1,000 person-years). Listing it is not to scare people, but to show that someone needs to be around during the "those days when I first learned about it."Long-term management, reimbursement and review after diagnosis are covered in Sections 16 and 24.

### 3. After losing your job, fix your schedule, medical insurance, and job search. Don’t stay at home all day.
<!-- Cost tag: Money=0 Time=less Perseverance=some Benefit=big Caliber=mortality rate -->
- Cost: 0, first apply for unemployment registration to receive unemployment benefits, and arrange job hunting into a fixed schedule
- Say it in plain language: Unemployed people are about 60% more likely to die in the following years than employed people. This is more obvious for men and people in the early and middle stages of their careers, and is highest in the first ten years after unemployment.Nearly a quarter of this gap can be explained by behaviors such as smoking and drinking—in other words, some of it can be suppressed by oneself.The order of things is: first register for unemployment, collect unemployment insurance benefits (employee medical insurance will be paid by the unemployment insurance during the period of collecting, see Section 7), don’t let the medical insurance be cut off, and then arrange a fixed schedule for getting up, submitting resumes and meeting people.
- Benefit: 42 studies, 235 mortality risk estimates, random-effects meta-analysis of more than 20 million people: After adjusting for age and other covariates, the average HR for all-cause death among unemployed people is 1.63; higher among men than among women; significant effects for early and mid-career individuals, smaller effects for later years; highest risk in the first 10 years of follow-up, and then declines; in those studies that control for health-related behaviors, the average HR is 2 lower
4%
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). Losing life and livelihood: a systematic review and meta-analysis of unemployment and all-cause mortality. Social Science & Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2011.01.005>
- Note: Observational evidence, reverse causation cannot be eliminated - people with poor health are more likely to be unemployed.But the article "The effect of controlling healthy behaviors is 24% lower" gives an actionable direction.See Section 7 for what you can get when you are unemployed, where to get it, and how to renew social security; how much compensation you should get if you are fired, and how to resign voluntarily without a signature, see Section 19.

### 4. If a loved one dies by suicide, accident, or homicide, don’t expect to cope with it, take the initiative to seek professional help.
<!-- Cost tag: Money=0 Time=less Perseverance=some Benefit=big Caliber=mortality rate -->
- Cost: Starting from 0, calling 12356 is free; psychological outpatient service is the same as ordinary outpatient service.
- Say it in plain language: People whose spouses die by suicide are about 80% (male) and 70% (female) more likely to be diagnosed with a mental disorder within five years than the general population. It is also 70% to twice as likely as those whose spouses died of other causes. The rates of suicidal behavior, mortality, and need for social assistance are all higher.People who have lost a loved one to suicide are also more likely to feel alienated and ashamed, and therefore less likely to speak up - which is exactly the reason to take the initiative to find someone, instead of waiting to "get better."
- Benefit: National registry cohort of 6.7 million adults in Denmark, over 136 million person-years, 1980 to 2014: IRR for new onset of mental disorder within 5 years among persons widowed by suicide, 1.8 (95% CI 1.6–2.0) for men and 1.7 (1.6–1.8) for women (compared to the general population); compared with those widowed by other causes of death IRR for men1.7 (1.5–1.9), 2.0 (1.9–2.2) for women, the proportion of suicidal behavior, mortality and need for municipal assistance are also higher, and the risk of physical diseases such as liver cirrhosis and sleep disorders is also increased.Systematic narrative review of 57 studies: Bereavement by suicide is associated with increased risk of partner suicide, increased risk of parental psychiatric hospitalization after child suicide, increased risk of maternal suicide after adult child suicide, and increased risk of depression in children after parent suicide; people bereaved by suicide more often report feelings of rejection and shame than those bereaved by other violent deaths
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Erlangsen A, et al. (2017). Association Between Spousal Suicide and Mental, Physical, and Social Health Outcomes: A Longitudinal and Nationwide Register-Based Study. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0226>; Pitman A, et al. (2014).and suicide risk. The Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(14)70224-X>
- Note: After sudden, violent death (accident, homicide, disaster), rates of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, and grief stuck are higher than after illness, and recovery is slower (Kristensen P, et al. (2012). Bereavement and mental health after sudden and violent losses: a review. Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1521/psyc.2012.75.1.76>).For information on what to do if you have suicidal thoughts, see Section 1, Article 25.

### 5. In the first six months after being widowed, leave meals, sleep, medicine for chronic diseases and follow-up examinations to a fixed person.
<!-- Cost tag: Money=0 Time=less Perseverance=some Benefit=big Caliber=mortality rate -->
- Cost: 0, find a specific person (child, sibling, friend) to visit or call regularly
- Say it in plain language: Less than half a year after being widowed, the probability of death in the same period is about 40% higher than that of people who have not been widowed; after half a year, the probability of death drops to about 15%.Men are about 23% taller, and women are so small that it may just be a coincidence.Another analysis, which combined more than 500 million people, put the overall figure at about 23 percent higher, with men at 27 percent and women at 15 percent.This difference is not made up by "thinking about prescribing". It is made up by not stopping the medicine, eating on time, and going for reexamination when it is time. Therefore, it has to fall on a specific person, so don't expect yourself to remember it.
- Benefit: Random-effects meta-analysis of 15 prospective cohorts, 2,263,888 people: RR 1.41 (95% CI 1.26–1.57) for death less than 6 months after widowhood, 1.14 (1.10–1.18) after 6 months; RR 1.23 (1.18–1.28) for men, women1.04 (1.00–1.08, not statistically significant); there is no significant difference between those younger than 65 years old and those older than 65 years old.Another meta-analysis and meta-regression of 123 articles, 1377 risk estimates, and more than 500 million people: adjusted HR 1.23 (1.19–1.28), higher in men 1.27 (1.19–1.35) than in women 1.15 (1.08–1.22)
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Moon JR, et al. (2011). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023465>; Shor E, et al. (2012).<https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-012-0096-x>
- Note: This is an observational study and confounding cannot be eliminated (couples’ lifestyles and diseases are inherently similar).However, the time pattern of "much higher in the first half of the year than later" is consistent in two independent collections and deserves to be regarded as a window of concern.Men are at higher risk and are less likely to ask for help.If there is a widowed elderly person in the family, the accounts, guardianship and will should be sorted out according to Section 17.

### 6. After the death of a parent, tell the child the truth, let him attend the farewell, and do not send him to another place for a "change of environment."
<!-- Cost tag: Money=0 Time=less Perseverance=some Benefit=big Caliber=mortality rate -->
- Cost: 0. The cost is that you have to explain clearly to your children when you feel uncomfortable too.
- Say it in plain language: Children who lose their father or mother before the age of 18 are about 50% more likely to die by early adulthood than those who have never experienced it; their parents are about 80% more likely to die from unnatural causes such as accidents and suicides, and about 30% more likely to die from diseases.So this is not something that "children forget in two days", the impact will last for many years.What you can do: Tell the truth and don't make up "he's gone on a far journey"; let him attend the farewell; don't interrupt his schooling or send him to a relative's house for a "change of environment"; put custody and parenting arrangements in writing.
- Benefit: Born between 1968 and 2008 in Denmark and 1973 and 2006 in Sweden
The entire population and 89.3% of those born in Finland between 1987 and 2007 were sampled, totaling over 7.3 million people, of whom 189,094 lost a parent before age 18: all-cause mortality was 50% higher than among those who were not bereaved (mortality ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.43–1.58); those whose parents died of unnatural causes1.84 (1.71–2.00), 1.33 (1.24–1.41) for those who died of natural causes; highest when the child’s cause of death is in the same category as the parent’s cause of death
- Evidence level: B
- Source: Li J, et al. (2014). Mortality after parental death in childhood: a nationwide cohort study from three Nordic countries. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001679>
- Note: The reason for giving B is not because the number is not hard - this is the whole population cohort of the three countries, and the number itself is A-level - but because there is no direct experimental evidence between this number and the specific practice of "how to tell the child, whether to attend the farewell".The authors also note that there is a lack of data on the quality of the parent-child relationship and lifestyle after bereavement, suggesting that both genetic susceptibility and the long-term effects of bereavement may increase.Appointment of guardians and property arrangements are found in Section 17, and child support and schooling are found in Section 18.

### 7. If the grief is still there after half a year and you can’t live any longer, go to a psychiatry or clinical psychology department to register.
<!-- Cost tags: Money=little, time=medium, perseverance=some, profit=big, caliber=mortality rate -->
- Cost: Registration fee plus treatment fee; this type of treatment is done according to the course of treatment. In the original trial, it was done an average of 16 times in 19 weeks.
- Say it in plain language: About one out of ten bereaved adults will be stuck: for more than half a year, they will continue to miss the deceased to the point where they can't eat, go to work, or take care of the children. They are numb, shocked, feel that life is meaningless, unable to accept, avoid everything related to the deceased, and can't recognize who they are.This is not "not strong enough". It has diagnostic criteria and specialized treatment: the effective rate of targeted treatment is 51%, and the effective rate of ordinary psychological treatment is 28%. On average, one more person gets better for every 4.3 people treated, and the effect is faster.
- Benefit: Systematic review and meta-analysis of 14 studies (non-violently bereaved non-psychiatric adults): Pooled prevalence of prolonged grief disorders was 9.8% (95% CI 6.8–14.0), with higher prevalence increasing with older mean age.Validity validation study of the diagnostic criteria (291 bereaved persons, three interviews at 0–6, 6–12, and 12–24 months): The core symptoms are intense longing for the deceased, plus emotional numbness, shock, feeling that life is meaningless, distrust of others, pain due to the loss, difficulty in accepting, identity confusion, avoidance of reality, and inability to continue living. At least five of the nine items occur daily or reach a disabling level, and are still present at least 6 months after death and accompanied by functional impairment.Randomized controlled trial of 95 people (16 treatments, average 19 weeks): treatment for complicated grief 51% effective, interpersonal psychotherapy 28% (P=0.02), faster onset of action, number needed to treat 4.3
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Lundorff M, et al. (2017). Prevalence of prolonged grief disorder in adult bereavement: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Affective Disorders. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.01.030>; Prigerson HG, et al. (2009).ICD-11. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121>; Shear MK, et al. (2005). Treatment of complicated grief: a randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.21.2601>
- Note: This diagnosis is called complicated grief or prolonged grief disorder in different literatures. Different standards require different lengths of time (6 months or 12 months). Don’t get stuck on the month - the judgment standard is "affects eating, sleeping, working, and taking care of children." If it matches, go and register.This prevalence rate of 9.8% comes from 14 studies with widely varying methodologies, and the authors suggest that external validity is limited.Departments are called psychiatry, psychology or clinical psychology, and they are available in public psychiatric hospitals.

### 8. Don’t spend money on grief counseling right away. First check whether you meet the requirements in item 7.
<!-- Cost tag: Money=0 Time=little Perseverance=No Income=medium Caliber=money -->
- Cost: 0, which in turn saves a consulting fee
- Say it in plain language: Universally arranging psychological counseling for all bereaved people will only have a small effect at the end of the intervention, and no benefit will be seen at the follow-up - the reason is that most people who do not receive intervention will recover on their own.In a study that tracked 205 people before and after they were widowed, the most common pattern was precisely the "resilient type", and only a few remained stuck.But for people who have obvious adjustment difficulties, the same treatment effect is as good as treating other psychological problems, and will continue to get better during the follow-up period.So the order is: first check item 7, and then spend the money if you are right. Don’t buy courses or consultations from the beginning.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 61 controlled studies: The effect size of bereavement psychological intervention is small at the end of treatment, and there is no statistically significant benefit at follow-up; the intervention that only recruits bereaved people who have "obvious adjustment difficulties" is as effective as psychotherapy for the treatment of other psychological problems; the poor results of studies without screening can be attributed to the natural improvement of the control group over time.Meta-analysis of 14 randomized controlled trials: therapeutic intervention was significantly better than control after treatment and follow-up, and the effect was still increasing during the follow-up period; preventive intervention was ineffective.Prospective study of 205 people from before to 6 and 18 months after widowhood: five patterns were identified (ordinary grief, chronic grief, chronic depression, improvement during bereavement, and resilience), of which resilience was the most common and ordinary grief was relatively rare
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Currier JM, et al. (2008). The effectiveness of psychotherapeutic interventions for bereaved persons: a quantitative comprehensive review. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.5.648>; Wittouck C, et al. (2011).<https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2010.09.005>; Bonanno GA, et al. (2002). - Note: This article says "not everyone needs to do it", not "don't do it all" - the high-risk bereaved people like Article 4 and the stuck people like Article 7 are exactly the groups that the evidence supports that it should be done.The conclusion of a Lancet review is the same sentence: Grief is not a disease, and most people do not need professional intervention. ### 9. After separation and divorce, don’t cut off medical insurance and don’t use alcohol to support it.
<!-- Cost tag: Money=0 Time=less Perseverance=some Income=medium Caliber=mortality rate -->
- Cost: 0, renew your medical insurance and stop drinking
- Say it in plain language: In an analysis that combined 11 countries and 6.5 million people, people who were separated or divorced were significantly more likely to die early than those who were still married, with the increase higher among men and younger people.The original article did not regard this as cause and effect - divorced people may have different health and income.Among the four mechanisms listed by the author, there are two that he can hold on to: interruption of income and security (don’t let medical insurance and physical examinations be interrupted), and deterioration of health behaviors (alcohol and tobacco).
- Benefit: 32 prospective studies, more than 6.5 million people, 160,000 deaths, more than 755,000 divorces, 11-country random-effects meta-analysis: Adults who are separated or divorced have a significantly higher risk of premature death compared with those who are still married; the increase is significantly greater in men and younger adults than in women and older adults; the authors discuss four possible mechanisms - social selection, resource disruption, health behavior changes, chronic psychological stress
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Sbarra DA, et al. (2011). Divorce and Death: A Meta-Analysis and Research Agenda for Clinical, Social, and Health Psychology. Perspectives on Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691611414724>
- Note: The original article gives "significant increase" and group comparison. This article does not include specific multiples. The income magnitude is defined as "relative decrease with only direction and no applicable threshold" as medium.See Sections 8 and 10 for property, debts and dowry upon divorce, and Section 18 for child custody arrangements.

### 11. In the first three months after the accident, all irreversible decisions should be postponed.
<!-- Cost tag: Money=0 Time=less Perseverance=some Income=medium Caliber=money -->
- Cost: 0, just postpone the decision at the cost of missing some opportunities
- Say it in plain language: After an accident, all kinds of "opportunities" and scammers are most concentrated: those who say they can contact the deceased, those who say they can help you get your money back, those who advise you to resign and start over elsewhere, those who advise you to sell the house quickly, those who want you to become a partner.Many decisions made during this period are irreversible - selling a house, resigning, investing the compensation money in one lump sum, hastily remarrying, and guarantoring for others.Set a hard rule for yourself: If the amount exceeds one month's income, or if you regret it and you have to pay a big price, postpone it for three months, and tell it to someone who has no interest in the matter first.
- Benefit: Avoid making irreversible property disposals during the stress period - selling your house, resigning, investing your pension or compensation in one lump sum, hastily remarrying, or guarantoring for others.The amount of this type of decision usually ranges from tens of thousands of dollars to the property level, which is why the income in this article is set as medium.The official caliber also lists "family changes, unemployment, and dropout of school" as psychological crisis risks that the community must proactively investigate, that is, the state during this period is considered to require external support.
- Evidence level: C
- Source: Author's experience, no direct literature; "Family changes, unemployment, and dropout of school" as psychological crisis risks are described in 25 departments including the National Health Commission (2026). Implementation plan for improving the psychosocial service system and crisis intervention mechanism (National Health Medical Administration [2026] No. 8). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065035.htm>
- Note: "Three months" is the threshold set by the author. There is no literature to support this specific length of time, so this article is grade C.See Section 17 (Investment for retirement, housing for retirement) and Section 6 (Fortune-telling) for common scams targeting the bereaved and the elderly; see Section 8 for the pitfalls of guarantees and IOUs.For the things that should be dealt with proactively - provident fund balance, social security benefits, and compensation for work-related deaths - see Sections 25 and 19.

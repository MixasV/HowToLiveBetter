# 5. Don't Waste Money

This section's dimension is money: saving percentages, annualized return differences, expected values. No conversion to lifespan or time. Entries involving investment only present the calculation method and verified historical data, not investment advice.

### 1. Turn off all auto-renewals; switch to manual renewal
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: One-time 10–20 minutes to review the "auto-payment" lists in Alipay/WeChat/Apple/Android; one extra tap at each renewal.
- Say it in plain language: What you save is the subscriptions you stopped using long ago but are still being charged monthly. Reviewing Alipay, WeChat, Apple, and Android auto-payment lists takes under 20 minutes; regulations require merchants to notify you prominently before charging and to provide a simple one-tap cancel option at any time.
- Benefit: Money saved equals the total of subscriptions "no longer used but still being charged" — varies by individual. Regulators require operators to notify prominently before renewal and provide "simple cancellation at any time"; canceling involves no extra cost (China, effective 2021).
- Evidence level: C
- Source: State Administration for Market Regulation (2021, 2025 revision). Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Online Transactions (Order No. 37, Article 18). <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2025/art_4b47c79b8d994a42bba4835997688faa.html>
- Note: For services you genuinely use frequently, annual billing is usually cheaper than monthly, so keeping auto-renewal is fine. This entry targets the "forgot it's charging" part.

### 2. Do annual tax reconciliation between March and June; fill in all applicable special additional deductions
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: $0; about ten minutes in the personal income tax app.
- Say it in plain language: People who worked only a few months full-year, changed jobs mid-year, or haven't filled in deductions for children's education, mortgage, rent, or elderly care often get a refund — about ten minutes on your phone. If total annual comprehensive income is under 120,000 yuan, or the tax owed is under 400 yuan, you can skip it.
- Benefit: After combining wages, labor remuneration, author's remuneration, and royalties, settle the difference. The State Taxation Administration's 2023 announcement extended to December 31, 2027: if annual comprehensive income doesn't exceed 120,000 yuan, or the tax to be paid doesn't exceed 400 yuan, reconciliation isn't required. Conversely, people with only a few months of income, mid-year job changes, or unfilled deductions (children's education, mortgage/rent, elderly care) usually get money back.
- Evidence level: A
- Source: State Taxation Administration (2025). Measures for the Administration of Individual Income Tax Comprehensive Income Annual Reconciliation (Article 6) and interpretation. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202502/content_7006465.htm>; Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2023). Announcement on Continuing the Policies on Individual Income Tax Comprehensive Income Annual Reconciliation. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202308/content_6900594.htm>
- Note: Those owing tax must also reconcile — skipping it is illegal. Special additional deductions include children's education, continuing education, major illness medical expenses, housing loan interest, housing rent, elderly care, and care for children under 3; mortgage and rent are mutually exclusive.

### 3. Housing fund isn't only for buying a house: rent, renovation, and property fees all qualify; under the new regulations effective September 20, 2026
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: Submitting one withdrawal application.
- Say it in plain language: The housing fund isn't locked to home purchases only. The new regulations list nine qualifying circumstances, including paying rent, renovating your self-occupied home, and paying property management fees. The center must answer with approval or denial within 3 days of accepting the application.
- Benefit: The revised regulations list nine withdrawal circumstances, including paying rent, renovating self-occupied housing, and paying property management fees for self-occupied housing; the management center must decide within 3 days of accepting the application.
- Evidence level: A
- Source: State Council (2026). Decision on Amending the Regulations on the Administration of Housing Provident Fund (State Council Order No. 844, passed at State Council executive meeting No. 93 on July 31, 2026, effective September 20, 2026): revised Article 24 lists withdrawal circumstances "(1) paying rent; (2) purchasing, building, renovating, or overhauling self-occupied housing; (3) repaying home purchase loan principal and interest; (4) renovating self-occupied housing; (5) paying property management fees for self-occupied housing; (6) retiring; (7) completely losing working capacity and terminating labor relations with the employer; (8) settling abroad; (9) other housing consumption circumstances approved by the State Council." Article 25: "The housing fund management center shall decide whether to approve withdrawal within 3 days of accepting the application." Article 18: contribution rate "shall not be lower than 5% of the employee's average monthly wage in the previous year." <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12946/202608/content_7079363.html>
- Note: The money in your account is your salary plus the employer match, sitting at a very low interest rate. The regulations also state: if the employee dies or is declared dead, heirs or beneficiaries may withdraw the balance. Materials and details vary by local center — check your local center's guide before going.

### 4. Recalculate your phone and broadband plans annually; downgrade what you don't use; complain if the carrier won't let you switch
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=some benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: $0; reviewing one bill takes about ten minutes.
- Say it in plain language: Old plans are often $3–15 more expensive than current offers — hundreds wasted per year. Carriers must not refuse or delay number portability without just cause, and can't set special rate plans for porting users. If they stall, complain.
- Benefit: MIIT regulations clearly prohibit carriers from "refusing, blocking, or delaying number portability services without just cause," from "using interception, restriction, or other technical means to affect communication service quality for porting users," and from "setting special rate plans or marketing programs for porting users." Old plans are often tens of yuan pricier than current ones; not touching them wastes hundreds per year.
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (2019). Provisions on the Administration of Number Portability Services (MIIT Letter [2019] No. 242). <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2019-11/13/content_5451483.htm>
- Note: First check your actual usage over the past six months in the store app, then choose a plan by usage. When customer service says "downgrading requires visiting a store" or "can't change during contract," ask for written justification; if they refuse, call 12300 to complain.

### 5. Don't buy lottery tickets
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: $0.
- Say it in plain language: By regulation, the minimum payout ratio for lotto/digit-type lottery is 50% of sales; Double Color Ball is 51% — every 1 yuan bet returns an average of 0.51 yuan in the long run, a stable 49% loss. In 2024, national sales were 623.486 billion yuan, of which 161.031 billion went directly into public welfare funds, outside the payouts.
- Benefit: Per Ministry of Finance rules, lotto/digit-type lottery prize ratio floor is 50% of sales, scratch-off 65%, sports lottery 73%; Double Color Ball allocates 51% of sales to prizes, so each 1 yuan bet returns only 0.51 yuan in expected value, a 49% expected loss. 2024 national lottery sales were 623.486 billion yuan, of which 161.031 billion yuan entered public welfare funds, not returned to buyers (China, 2024).
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Ministry of Finance (2015). Notice on Regulating and Strengthening the Management of Lottery Fund Composition Ratios (Caizong [2015] No. 94). <http://m.mof.gov.cn/czxw/201511/t20151113_1560814.htm>; Ministry of Finance (2025). MOF Announcement 2025 No. 8 (2024 lottery public welfare fund collection and distribution). <http://zhs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202508/t20250828_3970977.htm>; China Welfare Lottery Double Color Ball Game Rules (Article 14). <https://mzj.nc.gov.cn/ncsmzj/flcp/202111/5dccc1a5da89434e94a62f8ea0e5887e.shtml>
- Note: Negative expected value is the design outcome of the lottery system, not a luck issue. Treating it as a $0.30 entertainment purchase, not an investment, is the only reasonable framing.

### 6. Walk away from anything saying "high returns," "capital protected," or "guaranteed profit"
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=large dimension=money -->
- Cost: Zero; just stop when you hear these words.
- Say it in plain language: The regulators' own words: yields above 6% deserve a question mark, above 8% are dangerous, above 10% means prepare to lose all principal. Legitimate wealth products can no longer promise guaranteed principal and returns; money lost to illegal fundraising is borne by the participants themselves — nobody backs you up.
- Benefit: Former CBIRC chairman Guo Shuqing's words at the 2018 Lujiazui Forum: "Yields above 6% raise questions, above 8% are dangerous, above 10% mean prepare to lose all principal." The new asset management rules require financial institutions to "not promise guaranteed principal and returns" and "not advance funds to honor redemptions when payment difficulties arise," meaning legitimate wealth management no longer offers principal protection. Losses from illegal fundraising are "borne by fundraising participants themselves"; after trouble, no one backs you up (China, 2018, 2021).
- Evidence level: A
- Source: Guo Shuqing's speech at the 10th Lujiazui Forum (June 2018), republished by Tianjin Local Financial Bureau. <https://jrgz.tj.gov.cn/ztlm/ztzl/jrjfwphjr/202006/t20200625_2744709.html>; People's Bank of China et al. (2018). Guiding Opinions on Regulating Asset Management Business of Financial Institutions (Yinfa [2018] No. 106, Articles 2 and 6). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5323101.htm>; State Council (2021). Regulations on Preventing and Handling Illegal Fundraising (State Council Order No. 737, Article 25). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5588815.htm>

### 7. Don't use minimum credit card payments; don't open installments or consumer loans for purchases
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=some benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: Zero; need to keep enough cash for full payment before the statement date.
- Say it in plain language: The minimum payment isn't a perk — it's a daily-interest loan, around 18% annualized, and the interest-free period is voided on the spot with the remaining principal accruing interest in full. Read rates by annualized percentage — the central bank has required card issuers to prominently display annualized rates and forbids showing only "daily rates."
- Benefit: Credit card overdraft rates capped at "daily 0.05%" with a floor of 0.7× before 2020, equivalent to 18.25% simple annualized (from 2021, changed to negotiated pricing between issuer and cardholder). After the minimum payment, remaining principal accrues interest daily at the full rate and the interest-free period lapses. The central bank has required issuers to prominently display annualized rates and "not display only daily rates or daily repayment figures" — check the annualized number before deciding (China, 2020 notice).
- Evidence level: C
- Source: People's Bank of China (2020). Notice on Promoting Market-Based Reform of Credit Card Overdraft Rates (Yinfa [2020] No. 327). <https://xining.pbc.gov.cn/zhengwugongkai/4081330/4406346/4693549/4159909/index.html>
- Note: 18.25% is computed as 0.05% × 365, simple-interest basis; monthly compounding would be higher. Some banks offer discounts; rely on the annualized rate shown on your own statement. When genuinely short on cash, prefer lower-cost regulated channels; don't let minimum payments roll compounding interest.

### 8. Don't tip streamers, top up games, or impulse-buy
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=some benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: Zero; a few minutes to disable password-free payment, set per-transaction and daily limits, and unbind stored payment methods.
- Say it in plain language: In 2024, China's domestic game revenue was 325.783 billion yuan across 674 million users — about $72 per person. Money spent is largely unrecoverable: the law only supports refunds for minors' purchases without parental consent; adults have no such provision. The easiest defense is disabling password-free payment, setting limits, and unbinding payment methods.
- Benefit: This is a significant expense: 2024 domestic game market revenue 325.783 billion yuan, 674 million users, about $72 per person (computed by dividing the first two). Spent money is mostly unrecoverable: only for restricted civil capacity persons (minors) making tips/recharges without guardian consent are courts to "support the request for return" when guardians request; adults have no such rule. Minors may only play online games on Friday, Saturday, Sunday, and statutory holidays from 20:00 to 21:00, one hour each day (China, 2020, 2021).
- Evidence level: B
- Source: National Press and Publication Administration (2024). China's Game Market Revenue Exceeds 325.7 Billion Yuan This Year, a New High (2024 China Game Industry Report by China Audio-video and Digital Publishing Association Game Committee). <https://www.nppa.gov.cn/xxfb/ywdt/202412/t20241216_877445.html>; Supreme People's Court (2020). Guiding Opinions on Properly Handling Civil Cases Related to COVID-19 Pandemic (II) (Fa Fa [2020] No. 17, item 9). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/230181.html>; National Press and Publication Administration (2021). Notice on Further Strict Management and Prevention of Minors' Internet Game Addiction (Guoxin Chufa [2021] No. 14). <https://www.nppa.gov.cn/xxfb/tzgs/202108/t20210830_666285.html>
- Note: Rated B because there's no direct measurement of "how much you save by not tipping," only aggregate numbers. Disabling password-free payment and setting limits is experiential (C). The original text of Civil Code Articles 19 and 145 is quoted verbatim in the next item. Not investment advice.

### 9. When a child tops up or tips with a phone, large payments by 8+ year-olds can be claimed back if the parents don't ratify them
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=medium willpower=some benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: $0; takes time for evidence collection and negotiation — platforms usually refuse first then refund; if negotiation fails, lawsuits take months-plus and legal fees are on you, so prepare materials first and go through the platform and 12315.
- Say it in plain language: Money a child 8+ spends is invalid as long as the amount is clearly disproportionate to their age and intelligence and the parents neither agree nor ratify — you can claim it back. Children under 8 can't make legally binding decisions on their own at all. If the legal guardian stays silent, the law counts it as refusal to ratify.
- Benefit: Minors over 8 are persons with limited civil capacity; civil legal acts disproportionate to their age and intelligence require legal guardian consent or ratification to be valid; those under 8 have no civil capacity and their acts are performed by legal guardians as agents. If the legal guardian makes no indication, it's deemed refusal to ratify.
- Evidence level: A
- Source: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code. Full text republished by the Supreme People's Procuratorate. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> Article 19: "A minor over the age of eight is a person with limited capacity for civil conduct; the performance of civil juristic acts shall be carried out by his legal representative or with the consent/ratification of his legal representative; however, he may independently perform civil juristic acts that purely benefit him or are appropriate to his age and intelligence." Article 20: "A minor under the age of eight is a person without capacity for civil conduct and all civil juristic acts shall be performed by his legal representative as agent." Article 145: "A civil juristic act performed by a person with limited capacity for civil conduct that purely benefits him or is appropriate to his age, intelligence, and mental health is valid; other civil juristic acts he performs are valid after consent or ratification by his legal representative." "If the legal representative makes no indication, it is deemed refusal to ratify."
- Note: The key is burden of proof: you must prove the child performed the operation, not you. Useful materials: recharge times concentrated during school or parents' work hours, device usage records, the child's chat and game records, and the child's own explanation. "Appropriate to age and intelligence" has no unified monetary threshold — several dozen yuan for cosmetic items differs from tens of thousands for tips. Easier upfront: set password-free caps on payment methods, enable child mode, and never tell the child the payment password. For adult impulse-tipping, see the "don't tip streamers" item.

### 10. Teach your child one sentence: anyone who asks you to take your parents' phone and operate it or report a verification code is a scammer
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=some benefit=large dimension=money -->
- Cost: $0; explain the six scam types below to the child and agree on "if you're scammed tell me first, I won't yell"; also set payment password-free caps, enable child mode, and don't tell the child the payment password.
- Say it in plain language: These six scam types vary in approach, but the final step is always the same action — getting the child to take the parents' phone to transfer money or read out a verification code. So one sentence is enough: whoever asks you to touch your parents' phone or report a code is a scammer. Add "if scammed, tell me first, I won't yell" so the child reports the same day — only then can the money possibly be recovered.
- Benefit: Quanzhou Anti-Fraud Center, together with Quanzhou Education Bureau, broke down six youth scam categories with different schemes but the same final step — getting the child to transfer money using the parents' phone: low-price private deals for game accounts/items (Xiao Wang transferred 260 yuan privately and was told "minor transfers are illegal, account will be frozen," used his father's phone to click a "verification link," and 30,000+ yuan was taken); "lifting anti-addiction restrictions" (threatened "pay or cancel, your parents go to jail," transferred 7,000 yuan with his mother's phone; the original text notes anti-addiction is an official setting with "no 'private unlock' channel"); tip refund scams (fake "official customer service" saying minor tips can be refunded, requiring "gifting in a designated live room with the parent's Alipay to get a refund"; the mother's account lost $11,000 before being blocked; "someone adding you to 'gift for refund' is 100% fraud"); impersonating classmates or teachers (voice-note name-dropping after account theft; joining class group chats during enrollment season as "teacher" collecting "500 yuan miscellaneous fee" with identical avatar/nickname, plus fake "paid" posts); celebrity stalking (lured into a "star fan group, claim a $130 red packet," first transfer $7.3 for "verification," then threatened "minors involved is illegal, platform will freeze 80,000, your parents go to jail," totaling $2,400 lost; "real police and lawyers never contact minors online for investigations, never ask for parents' phones"); low-priced second-hand platform items luring buyers off-platform for private transfers. The same material lists three red lines children shouldn't cross: don't lend/sell/rent bank cards or phone cards (cards used to move stolen funds can constitute helping information network criminal activity, leaving criminal records affecting exams and civil service); "phone relay" gigs are illegal; overseas "high-paying recruitment" may mean you're controlled the moment you arrive (nationwide, October 2025).
- Evidence level: B
- Source: Quanzhou Anti-Fraud Center (2025). First Lesson of School Year: Anti-Fraud is a Must-Learn for Youth, Avoid These 6 Traps! <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202510/t20251028_7026836.htm> (republished by Fujian Provincial Public Security Department)
- Note: Rated B because official sources give scheme breakdowns and case examples, no incidence rates or prevention-effect statistics. Distinguish from the previous item: money a child spent on their own tips/recharges can be claimed under the Civil Code; money transferred to scammers does not qualify — it follows section 8 item 2's police-report-and-stop-payment route, recoverable only if still in the account. The scammers' core threat script is "you violated rules, your parents go to jail" — explaining to the child upfront that this phrase itself is a fraud marker works better than interrogating afterward. "Tell me first if scammed, no yelling" must be genuine money guarantee — fear of scolding leading to concealment is the common cause of expanding losses in these cases. For the adult version of impersonating acquaintances and AI face-swapping, see section 8 items 3 and 4.

### 11. Decline extended warranties on electronics
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: Zero; bear the small-probability cost of out-of-warranty repair.
- Say it in plain language: Extended warranties typically sell for 10% to 50% of the product's price, while their gross margins run ten-plus times ordinary goods — Best Buy in 2003 derived over half its profits from extended warranties that were only 3%–4% of revenue. That markup is what you're overpaying.
- Benefit: Extended warranty pricing typically runs 10% to 50% of the product's original price; industry estimates cited in the study put average extended warranty gross margins at 50%–60%, about 18 times margins on ordinary goods; in 2003, extended warranties were only 3%–4% of Best Buy's revenue but contributed over 50% of its profits (US, 2000s retail data).
- Evidence level: B
- Source: Chen T, Kalra A, Sun B (2009). Why Do Consumers Buy Extended Service Contracts? Journal of Consumer Research 36(4):611-623. <https://doi.org/10.1086/605298>
- Note: Margin figures are the paper's citation of industry estimates (Business Week 2004), not the paper's own computation. Applies to ordinary people who can afford a one-time repair; people whose livelihood depends on one device without a backup should calculate separately. Phone screen-crack insurance should be priced individually by your own drop frequency.

### 12. Prefer generic drugs that pass consistency evaluation and centralized procurement winners
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=large dimension=money -->
- Cost: Zero; one extra question when getting a prescription or buying medicine — "is there a centralized-procurement/generic option?"
- Say it in plain language: The second batch of centralized procurement averaged 53% price cuts, max 93%; 11 batches covering 490 drugs by April 2026. Asking "is there a centralized-procurement or generic version" at prescription time can save a large amount on the same ingredients.
- Benefit: Second batch of state-organized drug central procurement averaged 53% lower prices, max 93%; as of April 2026, 11 batches covering 490 drugs have been conducted, with 1,695 drugs passing or deemed-passing consistency evaluation, about 2/3 of common clinical chemical drugs (China, official data 2020–2026).
- Evidence level: A
- Source: National Healthcare Security Administration (2020). Q&A on the Second Batch of State-Organized Drug Central Procurement and Use. <https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2020/1/17/art_38_2264.html>; National Healthcare Security Administration (2026). - Note: Controversy. Central procurement uses consistency evaluation as the quality threshold; the official position is therapeutic equivalence. Some clinicians question actual efficacy differences for specific products, but no citable systematic evidence exists. Ask your doctor before switching, especially for chronic long-term medications.

### 13. Set up family cost-sharing once in the medical insurance app; your employee medical insurance personal account can then pay for your spouse, parents, and children's medical care and medicine
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: $0; one family cost-sharing binding in the local medical insurance app or agency.
- Say it in plain language: With one binding, money in your employee medical insurance personal account can pay for your spouse, parents, and children's treatment and prescriptions. Without it, it only covers yourself, and the money just sits in the account.
- Benefit: Personal account can pay the personally-borne medical costs for the insured and their spouse, parents, and children at designated medical institutions, and personally-borne costs for drugs, medical devices, and consumables at designated pharmacies; the document also explores using it for family members' residents' basic medical insurance personal contributions.
- Evidence level: A
- Source: General Office of the State Council (2021). Guiding Opinions on Establishing a Sound Outpatient Mutual-Aid Mechanism for Employee Basic Medical Insurance (Guobanfa [2021] No. 14), item (5): "The personal account is mainly used to pay the insured's policy-scope self-paid costs at designated medical institutions or designated pharmacies. One binding lasts indefinitely; most regions call it "family cost-sharing" and it's set up in the local medical insurance app. Note the reverse red line: personal accounts can't fund fitness or wellness consumption; cashing out through health-product purchases at pharmacies is insurance fraud.

### 14. Drink boiled tap water; don't buy bottled water year-round
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: Time to boil water and a little gas/electricity; one kettle.
- Say it in plain language: Beijing's first-tier water rate comes to $0.00075 per liter; bottled water at $0.15 per 500 ml is $0.3 per liter — a 400x difference. Tap water complies with a mandatory national standard; boiling is normally fine. When there's a real source incident, foul odor, or discoloration, boiling a few more minutes won't fix it — stop using it and report it.
- Benefit: Beijing residents' first-tier water price is $0.75/cubic meter, i.e., $0.00075/liter; bottled water at $0.15/500 ml is $0.3/liter, 400x tap water. Drinking water complies with mandatory national standard GB 5749-2022 (published March 15, 2022, effective April 1, 2023, administered by the National Disease Control and Prevention Administration) (China, 2015 water price and 2022 standard).
- Evidence level: C (disputed)
- Source: Beijing Municipal Commission of Development and Reform. Residential Water Price Table. <https://fgw.beijing.gov.cn/bmcx/djcx/jzldj/202003/t20200331_1752796.htm>; State Administration for Market Regulation National Standards Full Text Public System. GB 5749-2022 Hygienic Standard for Drinking Water. <https://openstd.samr.gov.cn/bzgk/std/newGbInfo?hcno=99E9C17E3547A3C0CE2FD1FFD9F2F7BE>; Hangzhou Joint Investigation Team (2025). Notice on Investigation into Water Supply Abnormalities in Some Hangzhou Yuhang District Communities (July 23, 2025), republished by Xinhua <http://www.news.cn/20250723/1be62dac3b0b4461a643791ff552e93d/c.html>; Hangzhou Yuhang District Government. Yuhang District 2025 April/May/June Source Water Quality Monitoring Data (published by Hangzhou Yuhang Water Holdings Group, testing unit Hangzhou Jiashu Environmental Monitoring Co., item 24 "fecal coliforms" in Table 1) <https://www.yuhang.gov.cn/art/2025/5/8/art_1229204072_59140140.html>, <https://www.yuhang.gov.cn/art/2025/6/5/art_1229204072_59142885.html>, <https://www.yuhang.gov.cn/art/2025/7/3/art_1229204072_59146464.html>; Public Security Ministry Cybersecurity Bureau (2025). Man Fabricated Fake "Police Situation Notice" and "Fecal Water" Rumor, Detained Criminally. <http://www.news.cn/politics/20250722/3178b3bd189c4eb4b293982e92aab438/c.html>
- Note: The controversy is that "compliance" governs plant-out water and piped terminal water; source and network incidents can briefly breach it. On July 16, 2025, Hangzhou Yuhang Renhe water plant source water had abnormal odor, and some Renhe/Liangzhu communities' tap water smelled foul; the official conclusion was sulfur-ether compounds from anaerobic algal degradation; after switching source, plant-out water showed no total coliforms or E. coli and full analysis met GB 5749-2022; seven officials were held accountable, July water fees waived, and filters replaced free. Meanwhile, fecal coliforms in the same source had been climbing monthly (Tiaoxi Yongsheng intake: 10/L in April, 7,300/L in May, 16,000/L in June, exceeding GB 3838 Class III limit of 10,000/L; the same month Siling reservoir 28,000/L and Mantoushan reservoir 24,000/L also exceeded). So: boiling only kills microbes — it doesn't remove odor substances or dissolved chemicals. If you smell odor or see yellowing/cloudiness, stop using it, report to the water company and 12345, temporarily switch to bottled water. Don't expect longer boiling to solve it. But this is a localized occasional incident with per-incident compensation, and still doesn't justify year-round bottled water. The viral "fecal water pipe mix-up" was a forged police notice; its author was criminally detained (see section 9). Bottled water at $0.15/500 ml is a hypothetical price for calculation — recalculate with your city's real price. For old communities with substandard secondary water supply facilities, install filtration or buy water only in that context. Water prices vary by region; Beijing is for magnitude reference.

### 15. Don't trade stocks frequently
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=some benefit=large dimension=money -->
- Cost: Zero; requires restraining the urge to act on every headline.
- Say it in plain language: Across 66,000 accounts, the most frequent traders earned only 11.4% annualized versus the market's 17.9% over the same period. Nearly all of that 6+ point gap was eaten by trading costs — the two groups' gross returns were actually similar; every move costs money.
- Benefit: Study of 66,465 US discount-broker household accounts (1991–1996): the most frequent traders earned 11.4% annualized vs the market's 17.9%, average household 16.4%; average household annual turnover 75%. Gross returns of heavy and light traders differed little; the gap came mainly from trading costs (US, 1991–1996).
- Evidence level: A

### 16. Don't borrow to invest, don't use leverage, don't buy what you don't understand
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=some benefit=large dimension=money -->
- Cost: Zero; requires resisting "borrow a little to earn more" during bull markets.

### 18. Among similar funds, pick the lowest fee
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=large dimension=money -->
- Cost: Zero; one extra look at "management fee + custody fee + sales service fee" before subscribing.
- Say it in plain language: Each 1 percentage point higher annual fee means about 18% less received after 20 years and about 26% less after 30 years. Among similar funds choosing the lowest fee is the only variable you can determine in advance in this matter.
- Benefit: Fee differences erode by compounding: 1 percentage point higher annual fee reduces terminal value by 0.99^20 ≈ 18.2% after 20 years and about 26% after 30 years. - Evidence level: C

- Note: The compounding formula is pure arithmetic assuming equal pre-fee returns between two funds; in practice, also count subscription fees, redemption fees, and Class C sales service fees. Not investment advice.

### 19. Don't put all your money in one stock, one platform, or one house
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=some benefit=large dimension=money -->
- Cost: Zero; accepting "you won't happen to hit the most profitable one."
- Say it in plain language: As long as assets aren't perfectly correlated, spreading across several reduces volatility at the same expected return. Conversely, all-in on one stock, platform, or house means its failure is your total failure.
- Benefit: As long as assets aren't perfectly correlated, diversification lowers variance at unchanged expected return — the basic portfolio selection conclusion from Markowitz 1952, used for decades since. Conversely, everything in one stock or platform means its variance is your entire variance, with no hedging when it fails.
- Evidence level: B
- Source: Markowitz, H. (1952). Portfolio Selection. The Journal of Finance, 7(1), 77–91. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1952.tb01525.x>
- Note: Rated B because the classic literature is theoretical derivation without empirical "how much loss diversification saves" numbers. Diversification only reduces volatility; it doesn't guarantee no loss. Buying broad index funds (item 17 in this section) is the easiest diversification. Not investment advice.

### 24. Don't stock up on "strikethrough prices" and big-promotion inventory
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=some benefit=medium dimension=money -->
- Cost: Forgoing some genuine discounts; requires remembering usual prices of regularly bought items.

### 28. Before prepaying your mortgage, do the comparison, not the gut feeling
<!-- cost_tag: money=0 time=low willpower=no benefit=large dimension=money -->
- Cost: Half an hour of math; requires honestly estimating the stable after-tax yield you can obtain.
- Say it in plain language: Prepaying gets you a fixed return numerically equal to your mortgage rate. Under equal principal-and-interest, early periods carry heavier interest share, so earlier prepayment saves more — verify with the bank app's "prepayment trial calculation" (China, 2026 rate).
- Evidence level: C
- Source: China Foreign Exchange Trade System authorized by the People's Bank of China (2026). Loan Prime Rate (LPR) announcement for August 20, 2026. <https://www.chinamoney.com.cn/chinese/rdgz/20260820/3399885.html>

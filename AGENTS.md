# High Cost-Effectiveness Life Guide — Project Rules

These rules apply to all work in this repository and must be followed.

## Project Positioning
- This is a life guide sorted by "cost-effectiveness." It follows the quantified style of geekan/HowToLiveLonger, but covers a broader scope: not only longevity, but four resources — lifespan, time and energy, money, and personal freedom; not only staying alive, but also money, law, work, family, and parenting.
- Every piece of advice must answer two questions: What does it cost (money/time/energy/money)? What do you get in return (change in overall mortality / reduction in specific causes of death / time and energy saved / money saved / protection and personal freedom).
- Sorting principle: Cost-effectiveness first, not by category. Entries with "low cost, high benefit" go first.

## Who Gets the Benefit (Beneficiary Scope)
- The whole book is cost-benefit calculated. Beneficiaries are divided into four tiers, ranked by "expected chance of the benefit returning to you": ① **Yourself**; ② **Spouse and direct relatives** (parents, children, grandparents, grandchildren); ③ **Friends, colleagues, and other relatives** — reciprocal relationships, helping others now may come back later; ④ **Strangers** — lowest tier, but not zero: the probability of return is low, and you don't know the other person's character, with risks of being scammed, dragged into trouble, or retaliated against.
- Different tiers of benefits are not combined. When writing for tier ④, both risks and benefits must be clearly stated (being scammed, getting dragged into cases, retaliation). Don't only write benefits, and don't write "strangers should be ignored."
- Entries involving others should be written in this order: ① First write what to do when it affects yourself or family — this is the main line and the main body of the "Benefit" column; ② Then write the differences for friends/colleagues and strangers, focusing on self-protection actions and disclaimers (don't take risks, don't intervene physically, don't move things arbitrarily, don't call the police but also don't commit illegal acts); ③ In the notes, clarify which tier the beneficiaries of this entry mainly fall into.
- The beneficiary tier does not change the magnitude of benefits (the magnitude is still mechanically matched to the entry's own "Benefit" column). It only affects whether it's worth doing, which is sufficient to note in the remarks.

## When Writing "Legal Support" Entries, Process Costs Must Be Written
- For any entry that says "legal support," "you can require," or "will ultimately rule in your favor," only writing the result without the process treats the win rate as benefit. You must also explain: Do you need to sue? How long approximately (ordinary procedure starts at 6 months, can be extended; summary procedure is 3 months)? Who pays the lawyer's fees (lawyer's fees are not included in the "litigation costs" listed in Article 6 of the "Interim Provisions on the Payment of Litigation Fees," and Article 29's "losing party bears the costs" does not include it).
- Similarly, when citing a disclaimer or evidence rule to reassure readers, make clear which segment it applies to: applies to the judgment result, not the same as "being dragged in"; applies to civil, not the same as administrative or criminal. Before writing, ask: "Is the risk covered by this law the same risk the reader actually fears?"

## Evidence Grading (Every Entry Must Be Tagged)
- A: Has quantifiable evidence, from meta-analysis, large cohorts, or RCT, can give specific numbers (such as HR, RR, percentage decrease)
- B: Has research support but difficult to quantify, or evidence from small samples/single study
- C: Author's experience or common consensus, no direct literature
- Entries with controversial A/B grades must mark "dispute" and list opposing evidence

## Citation Rules
- Only cite original literature: journal papers (with DOI or PubMed link), WHO/CDC/National Bureau of Statistics and other official agency reports
- Prohibited from citing Zhihu, WeChat Official Accounts, Sohu, NetEase, Baijiahao and other secondary retelling
- Numbers must be findable in the original text; uncertain numbers should not be written, use "to be verified"
- Don't fabricate DOIs or literature titles from memory; if the original text can't be found, mark TODO, don't guess

## Entry Format
Each entry uses uniformly:
- **Advice** (one sentence, starting with a verb)
- Cost:
- In plain language:
- Benefit:
- Evidence level: A / B / C
- Source:
- Note: (disputes, applicable population, precautions)
- Below the title line, immediately follow an HTML comment `<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0|少|多 时间=少|中|多 毅力=否|些|是 收益=大|中|小 口径=死亡率|金钱|时间|自由 -->`, invisible when rendered on GitHub, used by index.html for online search and filtering by cost dimensions; maintain in sync when adding or modifying entries. Meaning: money 0=free or saves money, 少=tens to hundreds of yuan or tens of yuan per month, 多=thousands of yuan or long-term significant expenditure; time 少=within a few minutes or handy, 中=a few hours at once or weekly hours-level, 多=daily ongoing occupation; willpower 否=just do it once, 些=change a habit or endure a little discomfort, 是=long-term fight against inertia
- "In plain language" is translating the statistical measures in the "Benefit" column into one or two sentences of everyday language: HR/RR/OR are all converted to "reduced by about X%"; population and research design are not written, only the direction and magnitude that readers care about are left; HR, RR, OR, CI, cohorts, meta-analysis and other words are not allowed to appear, and numbers not in the "Benefit" column are not allowed to appear. The "Benefit" column retains all numbers and confidence intervals as-is, not a single character changed. Entries with monetary and freedom dimensions are similarly written, translated into "how much money can be recovered / what consequences will be incurred."
- Benefit and dimension are two additional fields added later, answering "Is it worth doing" (evidence level only answers "whether the numbers are credible," and the two are orthogonal). The dimension is determined by what this entry mainly exchanges for: mortality (including health endpoints, survival rate), money (including social security benefits), time (including energy), freedom (including legal liability, personal information). **Different dimensions are not compared.**
- The magnitude of benefits should be matched mechanically to thresholds based on the entry's own "Benefit" column, not by feeling: mortality dimension looks at relative reduction (≥20% large / 10–20% medium / <10% or only alternative endpoints as small); money looks at amount (ten-thousands as large / hundreds to thousands as medium / dozens as small); freedom looks at consequences (avoid criminal liability as large / avoid detention or administrative penalties as medium / avoid civil disputes as small); time looks at savings (daily hours-level as large / weekly hours-level as medium / one-time as small). When numbers are insufficient to determine, use judgment, and note in the verification log why the level was determined.
- When the total number of entries and A-level entries change, synchronize in four places: README (body numbers and two badges), index.html (five descriptions, numberOfPages, header entry count), CLAUDE.md directory summary, and **tools/og.html + re-screenshot og.png** (the command is written in the header comment of the og.html file; the number of links in the cost-effectiveness dimension is the total number of http(s) in all "- Source:" and "- Note:" lines under book/). This last place is the easiest to miss.
- Cost-effectiveness tiers are not written in README, generated in real-time by index.html based on "benefit magnitude + three costs" (large benefit and all costs zero=extremely high, large benefit and cost points ≤2 or medium benefit and all costs zero=high, otherwise=average). Only the categorization rules are changed in index.html.

## Writing Rules
- English
- Reserved tone, no preaching, no exclamation marks
- The entire text is divided by dimension: mortality-related numbers, energy/time-related numbers, and monetary numbers, no cross-dimension conversion
- Write in plain language: the body text does not use abbreviations like ACM, all written as "overall mortality rate"; statistical abbreviations that must be used (HR, RR, CI, RCT, etc.) are explained in the README "How to Read the Numbers (Glossary)," new terms are added to the glossary first before use; law article clause numbers go in the "Source" column, the body text first says how to do it
- A single entry must not exceed 10 lines; long explanations go to separate files in docs/

## Work Style
- Can make changes across multiple sections at once, no need to stop after completing one section; report changes section by section
- Don't proactively expand the directory structure; if you need to add a new section, ask first
- When modifying existing entries, preserve the original evidence level and source, unless there is better original literature to replace

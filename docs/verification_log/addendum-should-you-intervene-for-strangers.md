# Addendum: Whether to Step In When a Stranger Is in Trouble · Verification Record (2026-09-09)

Task source: the reader asked "On the road, if you encounter a stranger calling for help, should you save them or not — encountering drowning, encountering someone collapsed with convulsions, encountering a group of people fighting, encountering an elderly person who has fallen, should you help them up".

Existing coverage: two of the five questions had already been written — drowning in Section 13 (originally item 25, now item 26), and collapse with convulsions in the original item 14 (now item 15), both of which wrote the on-scene actions. The real blank is the layer of "whether to step in": searching the whole book for "acting courageously for justice" appears only once in Section 7, item 3 (scope of legal aid), and searching for "184" and "good Samaritan" returns zero hits (the remark of Section 13, item 1 mentioned Article 184 of the Civil Code once, without an independent landing point), and "helping up" and "how to handle after a fall" return zero hits — Section 1, item 13 only discusses how to prevent yourself from falling. Witnessing others fighting also returns zero hits: Section 8, item 9 discusses being drawn into a conflict yourself and does not cover bystanders. Who to seek money from after being injured while saving someone is absent from the whole book.

Placement: Section 13 adds 3 items. The new item 2 (what to do when someone collapses, or an elderly person falls) is inserted after the cardiac arrest item, and the original items 2 to 36 shift to 3 to 37; the new item 38 (witnessing a fight) is inserted after "being extorted by a stranger" and before HIV blocking, and the original item 37 (HIV) shifts to item 39; the new item 40 (money after being injured while acting courageously for justice) is placed at the end. It is placed in Section 13 rather than Section 8 because these three items answer what to do first in those tens of seconds on the scene, and the legal provisions here are the basis for "whether you dare to act", not an independent topic.

Set at level B: the guidelines give handling approaches, without an effect size for "how many fewer deaths if followed"; the sentence "leading cause" is an epidemiological conclusion, but the actions advocated by this item (ask first, do not move, call 120 on the spot) have no controlled study. Benefit magnitude "large" — mortality approach; incorrect moving points to hard endpoints such as aggravated cerebral hemorrhage and spinal injury, and the guidelines list falls as the leading cause of injury death in that age group. Cost money=0 time=little willpower=some (must overcome the fear of being extorted and the fear of delay), composite "high".

Not written in the main text: the judgment text of the "Peng Yu case" circulating online was not obtained in its original form this round, so it is not written; the local regulations of various places' "good Samaritan laws" (such as the pre-hospital medical emergency regulations of Shenzhen and Shanghai) were not verified place by place, so they are not written. The remark that "if someone flags down your car for help in a remote place at night, you may not get out of the car and report the location to 110" is a judgment, not a quotation, so it is not placed in the benefit column.

Supplement 3 (same day, after the four-tier beneficiary approach was set): the author questioned "is this item really suitable for strangers; running away directly has a higher benefit". After checking, the question was found to hold — the original item treated strangers as the default scenario and pressed the entire "fear of being extorted" onto Article 184 of the Civil Code, but Article 184 exempts damage caused during the rescue process and does not cover the accusation of "conversely saying you hit them", which follows the rules of proof. Changes this round: the subject in the title is changed to your own elderly family members and acquaintances, with a tail added, "if it is a stranger, at least do not physically move them"; the plain-language opening states the main line; the remark is split according to the four tiers, stating that walking away directly from a stranger is not illegal, and that if you stop, do only the two zero-cost things of "not physically moving them" and "shouting from a distance, calling 120, reporting the location, and leaving", and do not lift the person into a car alone or pay money to send them to the hospital. A new source was added, checked verbatim on the Supreme People's Court official website <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/353651.html> (adopted 2014-12-18, first amended 2020-12-23, second amended 2022-03-22, effective 2022-04-10): Article 90 of the "Interpretation of the Supreme People's Court on the Application of the Civil Procedure Law of the People's Republic of China" — "The party shall provide evidence to prove the facts on which his claim is based or the facts on which he refutes the other party's claim, unless otherwise provided by law. Before a judgment is made, if the party fails to provide evidence or the evidence is insufficient to prove his factual claim, the party bearing the burden of proof shall bear the adverse consequences."

Sought but not found: among the top ten typical civil cases promoting the core socialist values released by the Supreme People's Court on 2020-05-13, there is no case of "being sued for helping up a fallen elderly person and the claim being dismissed for insufficient evidence" (the closest one on the official Q&A page <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/229251.html> is "the case of claiming compensation for sudden death after being stopped for injuring a child and leaving", which is a different situation), and secondhand articles say there is one, so it is not cited. Therefore the main text lands only on the burden-of-proof rule in Article 90 and does not write a case.

Supplement 4 (same day): the author again questioned that the minimum combination of "not physically moving them + calling 120 and leaving" is still a negative benefit for strangers, because calling 120 leaves a phone number and may lead to a follow-up call, and walking away is more cost-effective. After checking, this calculation was found to hold, and the original "at least do …" in the item was assigning a task rather than calculating an account, so it was changed this round: the tail of the title is changed from "if it is a stranger, at least do not physically move them" to "for the stranger tier, walking away is also lawful; if you stop, do not physically move them"; the cost column states that calling 120 leaves a phone number and may lead to a call back or questioning; the remark acknowledges that walking away is the lowest-cost option and that ordinary people have no duty to rescue, and narrows the only action with no second answer to "if you decide to stop, do not move them", and corrects the previous version — the reason for not moving them is medical (aggravating cerebral hemorrhage or spinal injury), not legal, since damage caused by rescue is already exempted by Article 184 of the Civil Code, and the previous version's writing of "only if you touch them is there the claim that 'you hit me'" turned a real-world probability into a legal consequence.

At the same time, an exception that makes "walking away" no longer free was added, checked verbatim in the SPP repost of the full text of the Civil Code <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>: Article 1198 "The operators and managers of business premises and public places such as hotels, shopping malls, banks, stations, airports, sports venues, and entertainment venues, or the organizers of mass activities, who fail to fulfill their safety guarantee obligations and cause damage to others, shall bear tort liability. Where damage is caused by the act of a third party, the third party shall bear tort liability; where the operator, manager, or organizer fails to fulfill the safety guarantee obligation, it shall bear corresponding supplementary liability." That is, if the reader is the operator, manager, or activity organizer of that place, walking away carries a risk of tort liability, which is a different tier from that of a passerby.

Set at level B: the provisions on the consequences are hard, but the step of "retreat and call the police, do not physically pull people apart, do not stand and watch" has no official original text and is inferred from the "active participation" in gathering a crowd to fight and the criteria for determining mutual fighting, as stated in the remark. Benefit magnitude "medium" — freedom approach; what is mainly avoided is the tier of detention and administrative punishment (only truly entering "actively participating in gathering a crowd to fight" reaches criminal liability, but that requires using your own hands, which is not the typical situation this item aims to prevent). Cost money=0 time=little willpower=some, composite "average".

Supplement (same day): the reader questioned "why not run, and will calling 110 get me pulled in for questioning and waste time". After checking, this time account was added to the item, and the source added the Criminal Procedure Law (2018 revision), checked verbatim in the full text reposted by the People's Procuratorate of the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region <https://www.nx.jcy.gov.cn/zwsy/qwfb/202011/t20201119_608817.html>: Article 62 "Anyone who knows the circumstances of a case has the obligation to testify. Persons who are physically or mentally defective or are young and unable to distinguish right from wrong or express themselves correctly cannot be witnesses"; Article 110, paragraph 1 "Any unit or individual that discovers criminal facts or a criminal suspect has both the right and the obligation to report or tip off the public security organ, people's procuratorate, or people's court"; the last two sentences of Article 111 "Public security organs, people's procuratorates, and people's courts shall protect the safety of reporters, complainants, and informants and their close relatives. If reporters, complainants, or informants do not wish to make public their names and the acts of reporting, complaining, or tipping off, they shall keep it confidential for them"; Article 193 "Where a witness, upon notification by the people's court, refuses to appear in court to testify without justified reasons, the people's court may compel him to appear, except for the defendant's spouse, parents, and children. Where a witness refuses to appear in court without justified reasons or refuses to testify after appearing, he shall be admonished, and if the circumstances are serious, upon approval by the president of the court, he shall be detained for not more than ten days." Note the article numbers: Articles 60, 108, and 188 of the 2012 version became Articles 62, 110, and 193 after the 2018 revision, and old article numbers are still circulating online; the full-text pages of the Criminal Procedure Law on the SPP site (spp.gov.cn) all returned the homepage when scraped this time, so a procuratorial organ repost page was used instead. The cost tag is still recorded as "time=little" — the default path is to make a phone call and leave, and being called back for a statement is possible rather than inevitable, and changing it to "medium" would not change the cost-effectiveness tier (benefit medium, and a cost score of 1 or 2 both composites to "average"), but the cost column text states that at most half a day may be spent. The remark points out that the benefit of this item to the reader himself comes from "not physically intervening and not standing and watching", and that calling 110 is altruistic, without pretending the two are the same account.

Supplement 2 (same day): the reader followed up with "That person on the ground has zero relationship to me, why should I care". This is a challenge to the premise of the item, and the way to handle it is not to defend but to write clearly the boundary of the self-interested part, so the remark adds another sentence, "not calling the police itself is not illegal". Checked in the SPP repost of the full text of the Criminal Procedure Law <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>: there is no crime in the Criminal Law punishing ordinary citizens for knowing and not reporting (only dereliction-of-duty provisions for persons with specific duties, which do not apply to ordinary citizens); Article 305 the crime of perjury "In criminal proceedings, witnesses, appraisers, recorders, and translators who intentionally make false proof, appraisal, records, or translations regarding circumstances important to the case, with the intent to frame others or conceal criminal evidence, shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not more than three years or criminal detention; if the circumstances are serious, shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not less than three years and not more than ten years"; Article 310 the crime of harboring and shielding "Whoever knowingly provides a hiding place or property to a criminal and helps him escape or makes false proof to shield him shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not more than three years, criminal detention, or public surveillance; if the circumstances are serious, shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not less than three years and not more than ten years". Although Article 110 of the Criminal Procedure Law writes "has both the right and the obligation" to report, there is no penalty provision, and the main text accordingly states that walking away is a lawful option. The benefit magnitude and approach are unchanged: the freedom-approach benefit of this item to the reader himself still comes from "not physically intervening and not standing and watching", and calling 110 is altruistic, as already pointed out in the remark and not merged into the same account.

Not used: no reminder page on the Ministry of Public Security official website (mps.gov.cn) about how to handle encountering a fight was found this round, so the main text does not write the words "public security reminder". No citable judgment document was obtained this round for the judicial case of "being recognized as mutual fighting after trying to break up a fight", so the item only says that the determination depends on whether hands were used, without giving a case.

"where it falls under the circumstance of being deemed a work-related injury, they shall enjoy a one-time work-related death subsidy and a special survivor subsidy equivalent to 40 months of their own wage"; "where the conditions for enjoying work-related injury insurance benefits are met, the corresponding benefits shall be implemented in accordance with the 'Work-Related Injury Insurance Regulations'; where they are not met, the civil affairs department shall assess the disability grade and implement the corresponding benefits"; "priority inclusion in employment assistance and key support", and public welfare posts "shall give priority to arranging eligible persons who act courageously for justice"; "priority inclusion in the housing security system, priority allocation or sale of affordable housing or payment of housing rental subsidies" |

Set at level B: the treatment approach is fixed by the national document, but the bonus standards and recognition procedures are set by each province's regulations on acting courageously for justice, and the figures differ by place (not verified province by province this round, so the main text does not write the bonus amount). Guo Ban Fa No. 39 does not have a dedicated chapter on the recognizing department, so the main text only writes "generally apply to the public security organ at the place of the incident or the work institution (foundation) for acting courageously for justice", and notes in the remark that this was not verified province by province. Benefit magnitude "large" — money approach; the one-time work-related death subsidy plus the special survivor subsidy of 40 months of wage is at the tens of thousands level or above. Cost money=0 time=medium willpower=no, composite "high".

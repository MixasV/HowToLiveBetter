# Addendum: Buying Counterfeit Goods and Image Copyright · Verification Record (2026-09-08)

Task source: the reader raised two real matters — "Many people sell things, and their employees bought counterfeit goods and were sentenced; what they sold was cosmetics"; "There are also clothes sellers who printed other people's copyrighted images and were arrested", and the reader believed that "he did not change it at all; I think at least he should change it a bit, add an icon, change a color, or something".

Existing coverage: the copyright part of Section 11 only discusses software, open-source licenses, and works made for hire (items 11 and 12), aimed at employed technical people; the remark of Section 12, item 15 has only one sentence, "before production, check the National Intellectual Property Administration trademark query system for similar registrations and then apply; this section has not verified the Trademark Law provisions, and this is only a reminder". For people doing business, the two red lines of "whether the goods purchased are counterfeit" and "whether the image used belongs to someone else" are absent from the whole book. Placement: Section 12 adds 2 items (items 16 and 17), and the original items 16 and 17 shift to items 18 and 19; the in-section reference "see item 17 of this section" (the exit item) is changed to item 19; the reminder in the remark of item 15 is changed to point to the new items. Nowhere outside the section is Section 12 cited by number.

**The reader's idea must be explicitly corrected in the main text**: changing the color or adding an icon does not constitute exemption. There are two legal bases: Article 52, item (6) of the Copyright Law lists "using the work by adaptation, translation, annotation, and other means" alongside direct reproduction as infringement; the Trademark Law directly writes into the infringement circumstances "using a trademark similar to its registered trademark on the same kind of goods, or using a trademark identical or similar to its registered trademark on similar goods, where confusion is likely to be caused" — "similar" itself is within range. Therefore the title of item 17 is written as "Changing the color or adding an icon does not count as 'having changed it'".

## Handling of the "MTF" half-sentence (a writing decision, not fact-checking)

The user's exact words included "follow the online MTF trend, not being defined by gender". This section does **not** write it as "don't follow the trend of transitioning", but rewrites it as item 7, "sex hormone drugs should only be used under a doctor's prescription and with regular follow-up; do not buy them online and do not increase the dose yourself", for three reasons:

1. What the evidence matches is the method of use, not the motive for use. Getahun 2018 measured the difference in vascular events between transgender women and cisgender controls; the conclusion it supports is "people who use estrogen need long-term vascular monitoring", not "should not use it". Writing it as a deterrent would be using this data to say something it does not say, violating this project's rule that "figures must be found in the original text".
2. A regulated medical pathway exists in China (assessment by endocrinology or the corresponding specialty, prescription, follow-up), and deterring people from it together with black-market channels would in practice push them toward the latter — which is precisely the highest-risk path.
3. The same rule naturally covers the other two items the user listed: self-increasing the dose of contraceptives / menopausal hormones / testosterone supplementation, and the anabolic steroids in item 5. Writing it as one line, "prescription + regular follow-up", is more accurate and shorter than classifying by population.

The introduction to this section states clearly that "no judgment is made about what anyone wants to turn themselves into", and limits the scope to the risk difference between the two paths of "prescription, qualifications, and follow-up" and "online purchase, unregulated institutions, and self-increasing the dose". The user's "feeling that you're ugly" is separately placed as item 8 (assess body dysmorphic disorder first), which is the only landing point in this part of the demand that has quantitative literature support.

## Explanation of evidence grade and benefit magnitude assignments

| Item | Grade | Basis for the assignment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Extreme dieting | A | Meta-analysis + large cohort, SMR 5.86 and 18 times are both figures from the original text. Benefit magnitude: mortality approach, the relative magnitude far exceeds the 20% threshold → large |
| 2 Two medical aesthetics certificates | A | The statutory figures are clear (three tiers of prison terms, 6/5/3 years of practice experience), consistent with the notation of the legal items in Section 9. Benefit magnitude: the "avoiding criminal liability" of the freedom approach here is for illegal practitioners, while for readers it is avoiding irreversible disability → recorded as large by health endpoint, approach recorded as mortality |
| 3 Facial fillers | B | Case aggregation, **no denominator**, so the probability of blindness per injection cannot be calculated, only the composition ratio. Benefit magnitude: blindness is irreversible and only 20.8% fully recover, the figures are insufficient to apply the relative reduction threshold, so it is set as "large" based on the irreversibility of the consequence |
| 4 Weight-loss products | A | SCOUT is an RCT with complete figures. Benefit magnitude: nonfatal myocardial infarction +28%, stroke +36%, exceeding 20% → large |
| 5 Anabolic steroids | A | Large matched cohort + replication cohort, HR 3.0. Benefit magnitude: far exceeds 20% → large. The dispute over the observational design and wide confidence interval has been noted in the remarks |
| 6 Weight-loss drugs via prescription | **B** | The statutory provisions can be checked verbatim, but there is no quantitative research on "how many fewer problems taking it by prescription causes compared with buying it yourself", so according to CLAUDE.md's "difficult to quantify" it is classified as B, and this reason is stated in the main text's remarks. Benefit magnitude: no figures to apply, judged as "medium" |
| 7 Sex hormones by prescription | A | Large cohort (4,960 people + 97,461 controls), risk differences and CIs complete. Benefit magnitude: only absolute risk differences (16.7 per 1,000 over 8 years), which cannot apply the relative reduction threshold; and monitoring can only reduce rather than eliminate risk, so it is set as "medium" |
| 8 Body image assessment | B | Prevalence meta-analysis, but the range across studies is extremely wide (2.21%–56.67%) and the tools are not uniform, so it is classified as B according to "small sample/large heterogeneity". Benefit magnitude: no endpoint figures, judged as "medium" |

## What was not done

- The association between social media use and body dissatisfaction was not written. Meta-analyses in this direction are mostly cross-sectional correlations (r values), with small effect sizes and unclear causal direction; writing it in could only land at level C, which does not match the evidence strength of the rest of this section. The user's "don't follow trends" was instead addressed in the section introduction by pointing out that "decisions mostly come from comparing yourself with others", without a separate item.
- The indications and abuse of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs (semaglutide, etc.) were not written. They change quickly, and the domestic indications and medical insurance rules are being adjusted, and item 6 of this section already covers the behavioral advice of "by prescription + doctor's judgment"; writing a separate item would require separately checking the current package insert and indication approval documents, so it is left for later.
- No official typical cases of illegal medical aesthetics were included. CLAUDE.md requires cases only for Section 9, and they are not necessary for this section; moreover, the Ministry of Public Security site consistently returns 521 on the local machine, so first-hand notices cannot be obtained.

# Addendum: Four Items on Extreme Violence as Venting · Verification Record (2026-09-08)

Task source: what the reader raised — "There have been a lot of violent incidents in China recently, reckless ones, someone who was owed wages went straight to the boss's door and killed him, and there are random car attacks on pedestrians, and a captain taking the whole plane down with him in suicide; these are all momentary impulses, harming others and themselves, completely zero benefit, just to vent anger."

Existing coverage: Section 8, item 9 only wrote "when a conflict starts, call the police first and do not use your hands", stopping at the level of public security punishment and minor injury, with nothing above that; Section 9, item 18 calculates the bill for fighting, but also only up to intentional injury. Searching the whole book for "revenge", "venting anger", and "endangering public safety by dangerous means", the only appearance of such a charge is in the high-altitude throwing item in Section 9, and there is not a single item on the side of "do not use your hands to kill". On the psychological crisis side, the whole book only has the self-harm direction: Section 1, item 25 (suicidal thoughts, restricting lethal means, 12356) and Section 3, item 19 (what to do first when feeling low), with no side on "wanting to harm others", and no "what to do when someone around you shows such thoughts".

Placement: Section 8 adds 4 items (items 10 to 13, inserted after the conflict item, progressing from having a specific object of resentment, to indiscriminate, to one's own impulse, to the impulse of people around, four items in progression), and the original items 10 to 24 shift to 14 to 28. Cross-section references are changed in sync: two places in Section 9 (items 12→16, 22→26), one place in Section 10 (12→16), two places in Section 12 (12→16, 22→26), one place in Section 17 (11→15), one place in Section 19 (13→17), and one place in Section 22 (23→27); the remark of Section 9, item 18 adds a sentence pointing to Section 8, items 10 to 13.

Regarding "a captain taking the whole plane down with him in suicide": the operable landing point of this kind of extended suicide for Chinese readers is not in aviation regulations (two people in the cockpit and such are matters for the company and the authority), but in "the people around knew but did not say", so it is written as evidence for item 13, not as a separate item.

Set at A (the legal provisions are verbatim-checkable, and the BEA report is the final report of an official accident investigation body). The 150 people is obtained by adding the crew and passenger numbers in the report; the report itself does not write this total separately, so the main text lists the composition.

What this item most needs to prevent is misreading, so the remarks set three boundaries firmly: it is only for real danger signals, not angry words; it cannot be used to send an disobedient family member to the hospital; and the law also provides that where re-diagnosis cannot determine a patient with severe mental disorder, inpatient treatment shall not be given. For what to do when unsure, two exits are given: first call 12356 to ask, or call 110 to have the police come to the scene.

The cost is recorded as money=little (registration and examination fees), time=medium (accompanying them to the hospital for a trip), willpower=some, so the cost-effectiveness tier falls in "average" — this item is not something that can be done casually.

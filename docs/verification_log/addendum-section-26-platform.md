# Section 26 "Building a Website or Platform" · Verification Record (2026-09-08)

Task source: the reader wants to build a live streaming website and a platform where users can post things for sale, and asks what procedures are required, what process to follow, and where to buy a server. The book's existing coverage had only the ICP filing and level protection in Section 11, item 14; everything else (value-added telecom license, EDI, network culture business license, audio-visual license, E-commerce Law platform obligations, tax-related information reporting, content governance, real-name, minors, notice and takedown, data export, platform collection and payment on behalf) had zero coverage. As agreed with the reader, a new Section 26 was opened, with 11 items, plus a long text docs/platform-permits.md.

Why open a new section rather than merge it into Sections 11 and 12: the reader of Section 11 is an employed technical person, and the reader of Section 12 is a person opening a shop and doing business; the person building a platform is neither, and splitting it would scatter the same matter across three sections with cross-jumps. The book's table of contents is scenario-oriented, and adding a new scenario does not break the format.

Working method: this round four retrieval subtasks separately looked for official original texts (telecom and filing, live streaming and audio-visual, E-commerce Law platform obligations, payment settlement), and the main task reviewed the key quoted passages before writing the items. The "Rechecked" column in the table below indicates whether the main task personally opened the original page to verify.

## Two outdated article numbers fixed in passing

The Cybersecurity Law was amended on October 28, 2025, effective January 1, 2026, and the order of the provisions was adjusted. The original text of the amendment decision includes "Article 59 is changed to Article 61". Accordingly:

- Section 11, item 14 originally cited "Cybersecurity Law (Articles 21 and 59)", which has been changed to Articles 23 and 61, with the old article numbers retained for comparison, and the URL changed to the amended full text published by the Central Cyberspace Affairs Commission <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm>. Article 23, item (3) "retain relevant network logs for not less than six months" was verified by the main task opening the original page.
- Another item in Section 11 originally cited "Articles 27 and 63", of which Article 27 (prohibiting illegal intrusion into another person's network) is now Article 29, which has been verified; the new article number of the original Article 63 in the legal liability chapter is not listed in the corresponding relationship table of the amendment decision, and could not be confirmed this time, so the source column truthfully notes "the article numbers of the legal liability chapter after the amendment have been adjusted, and this time they were not verified one by one".

## Could not be opened or obtained

- The WebSearch quota of 200 for this session was exhausted by the subtasks, and the later locating relied entirely on probing gov.cn gazette pages by number and the policy library search interface.
- No usable full-text page of the E-commerce Law was found on the Chinese government website (<https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5320991.htm> and <http://www.gov.cn/xinwen/2018-08/31/content_5318220.htm> both returned 404), so the item page of the National Laws and Regulations Database flk.npc.gov.cn was cited instead; that page is front-end rendered, readable in a browser but the main text cannot be scraped by a script.
- The official explicit text that "e-commerce platforms with matchmaking and fund processing functions must apply for an EDI license" was not obtained. The MIIT service guide only says "apply for the corresponding telecom business operating license according to the business definition", and it has answered that "ride-hailing platforms only need to do website filing" and "equity-type and bulk commodity trading platforms only need to do website filing". The item therefore only writes the B21 definition and does not draw a conclusion.
- The Two Supreme People's Courts and Two Ministries judicial interpretation or guiding case that directly characterizes "engaging in commercial internet cultural activities without a license" or "engaging in internet audio-visual program services without authorization" as the crime of illegal business operation was not obtained; the existing interpretation (Fa Shi [2000] No. 12) targets unauthorized operation of international or Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan-related telecom business.
- Official cases of being punished by the People's Bank of China or criminally sentenced for operating payment business without a license were not obtained, and the sampled batches of the PBOC administrative penalty disclosure column do not contain this type of violation.
- HTTPS handshake failed across the whole npc.gov.cn site, mps.gov.cn returned 521 across the whole site, and multiple provincial communications administration sites had handshake failures or 502.
- The two documents "Provisions on the Administration of Internet Live Streaming Services" and "Notice on Strengthening the Standardized Administration of Online Live Streaming Tipping" do not have document numbers marked on their official pages, and the source column only writes the issuing unit and date.
- The "Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture" forwarded by the Chinese government website in July 2025 published the unrevised 2011 original text, whose Article 21 still cites the already-repealed "Measures for Investigating and Banning Unlicensed Business Operations". This section uniformly uses the current text of the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, and this pitfall is recorded here for reference.

## III. Approach and benefit magnitude

- The long-term prescription in Section 16 is set as "approach=time, benefit=medium": what it mainly gains back is half a day of traveling to the hospital each month, not money; by the threshold of the time approach (hour-level per week is medium), it is set as medium.
- Signing up with a family doctor is set as "money, medium": what is avoided is a self-paid service package of several hundred to several thousand yuan.
- Fundus and foot screening is set as "mortality, large": the endpoints are blindness and amputation, which are preventable serious health endpoints.
- Long-term care insurance is set as "money, large": the payment ratio is 50%–70%, and the annual limit is tied to 50% of per capita disposable income, which is at the tens of thousands level.
- The two items in Section 14 are set as "approach=freedom" (the book's freedom approach includes personal information), but the three-tier thresholds for benefit magnitude are drawn by criminal liability/administrative punishment/civil dispute, which cannot be applied to personal information items. Both are set as "medium" by judgment: what they change is the long-term surface of information exposure, which neither involves criminal or administrative consequences nor is merely a one-time civil dispute. This is the only place in this round where the threshold was not mechanically applied.

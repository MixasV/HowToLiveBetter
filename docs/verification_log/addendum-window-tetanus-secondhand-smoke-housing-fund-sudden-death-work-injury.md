## III. About the item "if you make it to your workstation it counts as a work-related injury"

This item was added in response to a reader's question, and the source is a claim circulating in short videos: someone felt unwell but insisted on driving to the company before the illness struck, and the family received compensation; someone felt unwell at the company, went home and then something happened, and the company did not pay.

Checked against the legal provisions, this claim reverses half of the causation. Article 15 requires sudden illness "at work and at the workplace" — what is decisive is **where the person was when the illness struck**, not where the person died. Falling ill while on duty and then being sent to the hospital, and dying after ineffective rescue within 48 hours, may still apply; but falling ill on the commute is covered by Article 14, item (6) only for traffic accident injuries, not for illness, so "insisting on driving to the company" is not a sure-win move in law, and is clearly a bad choice in medicine.

Therefore this item is written as a correction item, with the approach set as mortality rather than money: the behavior it aims to change is "call 120 first rather than rush on the road", and the benefit magnitude is set as "large" by survival rate.

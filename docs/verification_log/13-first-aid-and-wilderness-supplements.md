# Verification Record: Section 13, 9 Added Items (First Aid Gaps + Wilderness)

Verification date: 2026-09-07. This round Section 13 goes from 18 to 27 items, and the book from 309 to 318 items.

What was added are several common fatal scenarios that had been missed: anaphylactic shock, epileptic seizures, hypoglycemia, electric shock, and carbon monoxide poisoning; and in the wilderness part, which previously had only getting lost, hypothermia, snakebite, animal encounters, and heatstroke, lightning strike, altitude illness, ticks, and wilderness drinking water were added.

The method is the same as in previous rounds: for English literature, Europe PMC REST (`search?query=TITLE:"…"&resultType=core`) was used to retrieve the bibliographic record and original abstract text, and for open-access ones `/{PMCID}/fullTextXML` was used to retrieve the full text for verbatim checking; official institutional pages were retrieved with WebFetch and quoted verbatim.

## III. Approach and benefit magnitude

Seven of the nine items have mortality as the approach (including health endpoints). The benefit magnitude is set according to Section 13's general approach: those that directly determine life or death (anaphylactic shock, severe hypoglycemic episodes, electric shock, carbon monoxide, lightning strike, high-altitude cerebral edema/pulmonary edema) are set as "large"; those that mainly avoid secondary harm or delay (epilepsy, ticks, wilderness drinking water) are set as "medium". Except for ticks (which require a pair of fine-tipped tweezers, so money = little), all are zero-cost, and lightning strike and altitude additionally record "willpower = some" — the hard part is not knowing, but being willing to turn back when thunder sounds and to descend on the plateau.

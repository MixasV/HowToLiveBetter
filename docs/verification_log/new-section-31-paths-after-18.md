# New: Section 31, What Paths Are There After Eighteen

2026-09-10. The reader's question was "Should I join the military, what are the benefits, how many choices are there in life, is it only working and studying?"

**This section was reworked once, and the reason is recorded at the front**: the first version wrote 10 of the 11 items all about joining the military, and the reader immediately pointed out, "Why did you write it all about the military? I was just giving an example." The problem was not whether the content was correct, but reading the question — what he asked was "what paths are there", and the military was just an example. After the revision, the positioning was changed to a path map: item 1 covers the legal thresholds of eight paths, the military was compressed into 5 items (by merging, with the content basically retained), and 6 more items were added to write about other paths, making 12 items in the whole section. **Lesson: the example the reader gives is not the question itself; before starting to write, first confirm which question is being answered.**

## Two corrections that must be remembered

- **The "Regulations on the Placement of Retired Soldiers" (State Council and Central Military Commission Order No. 608, 2011) was abolished on September 1, 2024**, and the current one is the "Regulations on the Placement of Retired Military Personnel" (No. 787), whose Article 93 states, "These Regulations shall come into force on September 1, 2024. The Regulations on the Placement of Retired Soldiers shall be abolished at the same time." The wording of the conditions for arranged work has changed: the old version was "non-commissioned officers who have served on active duty for 12 years" and "those who have been awarded a second-class merit or above in peacetime or a third-class merit or above in wartime", while the new version is "sergeants who have served on active duty for 12 years", "individuals who have received medals or honorary titles", "individuals who have been awarded a third-class war merit, second-class merit or above", and "individuals who have received a first-class commendation", plus grades 5 to 8 disability due to war and children of martyrs. Online guides generally still cite the old regulations.
- **The current standard for military service tuition compensation is 20,000 yuan for undergraduate and junior college students and 25,000 yuan for graduate students** (from the fall semester of 2024). The history: 8000/12000 (Cai Ke Jiao [2019] No. 19) → 16000/20000 (Jiao Cai [2023] No. 4) → 20000/25000 (Cai Jiao [2024] No. 188). The pair 12000/16000 never appeared in the search; encountering it means it was copied wrong. The Ministry of Veterans Affairs' 2022 policy summary still writes 8000/12000.

There is another key point: **the "Provisions on the Recruitment of Civil Servants" has no "targeted recruitment" clause**. After reading the full text verbatim and searching, the four words "grassroots", "targeted", "grassroots service programs", and "retired military" appear 0 times; the "Civil Servants Law" also has only one sentence, "higher-level organs shall pay attention to openly selecting civil servants from grassroots organs". The real basis for targeted recruitment is the 2017 "Opinions on Further Guiding and Encouraging College Graduates to Work at the Grassroots Level" issued by the General Office of the CPC Central Committee and the General Office of the State Council, and the hard 10% target in Ren She Bu Fa [2021] No. 32, and item 7 attaches its source accordingly.

# Addendum: What to Do After Being Cyberbullied · Verification Record (2026-09-09)

Task source: after splitting out the sixth layer (secondary spread and cyberbullying) of [docs/strangers-trouble.md](../strangers-trouble.md), the author asked whether to add a separate item on "what to do when you encounter cyberbullying".

Existing coverage: only the second half of the sentence "when cyberbullied, preserve evidence first and then call the police" in Section 8, item 14, whose main body is "do not become the party that spreads rumors and insults"; what can be done after being cyberbullied had only half a sentence. Searching the whole book for "cyberbullying" hits only this one item, and "personality rights infringement injunction", "doxxing", and "quick evidence collection" return zero hits; the "Provisions on the Governance of Cyberbullying Information" previously appeared only in the long text above, not in the main text of book/. The conclusion is that it is a real blank, so one item was added.

Placement: one new item 35 is added at the end of Section 8, not inserted after item 14. There are two reasons: first, the tail of Section 8 (items 30 to 34) is already the cluster of "what to do when you have been infringed upon" — being extorted for money, being falsely accused, the presumption of innocence, state compensation, and being the victim seeking compensation — and cyberbullying following them is the same category; second, inserting it after item 14 would require shifting 20 items and changing 8 external references and 11 "item N of this section" references, whereas appending at the end involves zero renumbering. A sentence pointing to item 35 was added to the remark of item 14.

Set at level A: all four are currently effective official documents, and the provisions themselves give clear executable actions and thresholds (which five circumstances transfer to public prosecution, and that a case shall be filed after assistance in obtaining evidence). Benefit magnitude "medium" — freedom approach; what this item gives the reader is stopping the infringement and pursuing the other party, at the same tier as item 31 (being falsely accused allows demanding accountability), and is not recorded as "large". Cost money=0 time=medium willpower=some, composite "average".

According to the rule set in this round that "when writing about legal support you must write the process cost", the remark states: the platform is the fastest and cheapest, and the court is the slowest and most expensive, so solidify evidence and report first; an injunction requires applying to the court, a private prosecution requires filing and proving the case yourself, and transfer to public prosecution requires meeting one of the five circumstances in Article 12, and if that cannot be met, only a private prosecution is possible. Two things not to do are also added: do not argue back (it will turn you into the one punished in item 14), and do not delete the content in your own account (that is evidence).

Not written into the main text: the quantitative evidence on the association between cyberbullying and suicide (the checkable meta-analyses are mainly on adolescents, and the population and situation do not match), with only the exit 12356 given in the remark (Section 1, item 25). The 12377 cyberspace report telephone number was not obtained in a verbatim-checkable official page this round, so the main text only writes "the quick report entry in a prominent position on the platform" and does not write the hotline number. Article 1195 of the Civil Code (notice-takedown) was not obtained from the SPP repost page this round and was not cited, and platform liability instead cites the "Provisions on the Governance of Cyberbullying Information".

# Section 29 "After a Major Blow" · Verification Record (2026-09-09)

Task source: the reader's exact words — "I feel something is still missing, like what to do when you hit a major setback in life, when a relative dies, some people get depressed because of this."

Existing coverage: scattered in three places, and none of them is about this. Section 1, item 25 discusses restricting lethal means and 12356, Section 3, item 19 discusses exercise and light exposure when feeling low, Section 22, item 7 discusses the dose of exercise for depression, and Section 25 as a whole discusses the procedures and money after someone dies. The health risks of bereavement itself, the time window of risk, when to go register at a clinic, mortality after unemployment and divorce, and how to arrange for children whose parents have died — zero coverage in the whole book.

Placement (confirmed with the user): **open a new Section 29**, file `book/29-after-major-setbacks.md`. The alternative was to split it into Sections 3, 25, and 1, which was not adopted: the common point of these items is the "time window" — the first 24 hours, the first week, the first month, the first half year — and if split up, this line cannot be seen, and readers facing a crisis would not find it.

## Several writing decisions

- **ICD-11 / DSM-5-TR inclusion of prolonged grief disorder was not written.** The original wording of Lundorff 2017 is "expected to be included in the forthcoming ICD-11", and Prigerson 2009 is "proposed for DSM-V and ICD-11", both only reaching "proposed/expected". No verbatim-checkable original text of ICD-11 or DSM-5-TR was obtained locally, so the main text was changed to "different literature calls it complicated grief or prolonged grief disorder, and different standards require different durations (6 months or 12 months); don't get stuck on the months yourself, go register at a clinic based on whether it affects eating, sleeping, working, or caring for children", avoiding this unverified claim.
- **Item 6 is given B rather than A.** Li 2014 is a whole-population cohort in three countries, and the figures themselves are level A; it was lowered to B because there is no direct trial evidence between "mortality is 50% higher" and "tell the child truthfully, let him attend the farewell, don't send him away", so it is an extrapolation.
- **The benefit magnitude of item 9 is set as "medium" rather than "large".** According to the project threshold, the relative reduction should be used, but the original text does not give a pooled ratio that can be applied, only the direction and group comparisons, so according to the rule "when the figures are insufficient to determine, use judgment and state the basis", here it is set as medium on the basis of "only the direction".
- **The approach of item 8 is set as money.** Its actual effect is "don't spend this counseling fee first", and the benefit falls on the money-saving side; the health endpoint of grief itself is already covered by item 7.
- **Directions not included**: the number of days of bereavement leave (Ministry of Labor Lao Zong Xin Zi [1980] No. 29). A search of the State Council policy document library with `searchfield=title` for "bereavement leave", "marriage and bereavement leave", "apply for marriage and bereavement leave", and "employee visiting relatives treatment" all returned zero hits; that library only contains current documents of the State Council system, the 1980 former Ministry of Labor notice is not in it, and no verbatim-checkable official full text was found locally, so per the project rules it is not written.

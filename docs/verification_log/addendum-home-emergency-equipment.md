## Benefit evidence

| URL | Result | Original quoted passage |
|---|---|---|
| Europe PMC REST, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD005014.pub3 | Opened, Kendrick D, et al. 2012 Cochrane review, 98 studies, 2,605,044 people | Injury incidence IRR 0.89 (95% CI 0.78 to 1.01); interventions carried out at home IRR 0.75 (0.62 to 0.91); interventions that did not provide safety equipment IRR 0.78 (0.66 to 0.92); safety behaviors: working smoke alarms OR 1.81 (1.30 to 2.52), escape plans OR 2.01 (1.45 to 2.77), safe storage of medicines OR 1.53 (1.27 to 1.84), safe storage of cleaning products OR 1.55 (1.22 to 1.96), stair gates OR 1.61 (1.19 to 2.17), socket covers OR 2.69 (1.46 to 4.96); "there is a lack of evidence that home safety interventions reduce the incidence of thermal burns or poisoning" |

Rating note: the item is set at B and marked as disputed. The reason is that on the benefit side there is only this one Cochrane review, its main result's confidence interval crosses 1, and the effect size of the "no equipment provided" group is comparable to the overall, indicating that the main source of injury reduction is not the equipment itself; the official list is an authoritative document but contains no effect size. The benefit magnitude applies the threshold by the relative reduction of the mortality approach: IRR 0.89 corresponds to about 11%, falling in the 10–20% range, recorded as "medium", not "large". The cost is recorded as a few hundred yuan per set, money=little, time=little, willpower=no.

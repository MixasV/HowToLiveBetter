# Addendum: Bulk-Registering Alt Accounts to Farm Platform Discounts · Verification Record (2026-09-11)

Task source: a type of case the reader saw in a short video — someone bulk-registered virtual phone numbers to impersonate new users, repeatedly claimed discounts like "the first two times are free for new users", and resold them for cash, farming more than 400,000 yuan from the company, and the person was arrested. The reader's example was free charging for a car company, but the question is the general one of "at what point does farming discounts become a crime", not a particular car company, so the item is written generally as "platform discounts", and the main text does not name a particular industry.

Existing coverage: Section 11, item 3 writes about "writing and selling scripts for ticket grabbing, flash sales, brushing orders, and farming discounts", with the landing point being the crime of illegally obtaining computer information system data under Article 285, paragraph 2 of the Criminal Law, and the subject being the technical person who makes the tool; Section 11, item 4 writes about crawlers and personal information; Section 8, item 7 writes about lending bank cards and phone cards (two cards); Section 9's original item 15 writes about not lending an ID card. None of the four writes about the side of "the user himself fabricating an identity or a transaction to fraudulently obtain a discount, handled as fraud", nor about the characterization of "discovering a system vulnerability and repeatedly using it". The placement is one new item in Section 9 (item 8), with the original items 8 to 19 shifting to items 9 to 20, and three cross-section references changed in sync (two in book/08, one in book/27).

Reason for the position: this section's ordering standard is "most easily stepped on unintentionally × heaviest consequences". This item follows immediately after item 7 (picking up a bank card or phone and not pocketing it); the two share the same psychology — "the thing came to my door by itself" and "the system gave it itself" — and the reader does not feel this is theft; but the consequences of this item are heavier: embezzlement carries a maximum of 5 years, credit card fraud a maximum of 10 years, and fraud reaching an especially huge amount carries not less than 10 years up to life imprisonment. It is placed before high-altitude throwing (the original item 8).

Version check of the provisions: **Article 295 of the Criminal Law cannot cite the 1997 text reposted by the SPP** — the 1997 original text is "if the circumstances are especially serious, shall be sentenced to life imprisonment or death", while the current text (after Criminal Law Amendment (IX) abolished the death penalty for this crime) is "if the circumstances are serious, shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not less than five years and not more than ten years; if the circumstances are especially serious, shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not less than ten years or life imprisonment", and the two differ greatly, so the three Criminal Law provisions in this item uniformly cite the consolidated text "amended according to Criminal Law Amendment (XI)" reposted by the Beijing Municipal Public Security Bureau Traffic Management Bureau, and have been compared verbatim on that page. Articles 266 and 253-1 in that consolidated text are consistent with the wording written into the item.

Case selection: for the car company free charging case mentioned by the reader, no verbatim-checkable notice or typical case was found in the SPP, Supreme People's Court, or Ministry of Public Security channels (mps.gov.cn consistently returns 521 on the local machine), so that case is not written, and instead three similar cases published by the SPP are used (e-commerce new-user coupons, online store refund vulnerability, ride-hailing subsidy order brushing), whose methods respectively correspond to "buying virtual numbers to bulk-register", "exploiting a system vulnerability to repeatedly claim", and "trading with oneself", covering all the elements in the reader's question. The magnitude of "more than 400,000 yuan in losses" mentioned by the reader has no accompanying case, and is only written in the plain-language section according to the tier in Article 1 of Fa Shi [2011] No. 7 as "causing the merchant to lose four or five hundred thousand yuan falls exactly in the 3 to 10 year tier", without fabricating a specific case.

Related provisions not written into the item (left for later): Article 287-2 of the Criminal Law, the crime of assisting information network criminal activities (upstream platforms that set up code-receiving platforms and sell verification codes are sentenced under this crime, as already seen in the Taizhou "Yi Ma" platform case published by the SPP); this item discusses the user side and does not elaborate on it; the administrative penalty tier for fraud in Article 58 of the Public Security Administration Punishments Law (2025 revision) has already been cited in Section 8, and this item does not repeat it.

# Addendum: How Emergency Department Triage Works, Foreign Objects Impaled in the Body and Fractures, Disability Assessment and the Disability Certificate

2026-09-10. The trigger was three things the reader raised: how the hospital visit process actually works (in the news, someone with a knife stuck in their head was still queuing at the registration window), what to do on the spot for major injuries and fractures, and whether to get a disability certificate for oneself. The check found that none of these three blocks had an existing item, so they were placed by attribution into Section 13 (on-scene first aid) and Section 24 (the medical visit process and the procedures after treatment is finished), without adding a new section.

Section 13 adds items 41 and 42; Section 24 adds items 8, 9, 10, and 11.

## Section 24, item 8 (emergency pre-examination triage)

| URL | Rechecked | Original quoted passage |
|---|---|---|
| <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202405/content_6951260.htm> (Notice of the General Office of the National Health Commission on Issuing the 2024 Edition of Medical Quality Control Indicators for Six Specialties Including Emergency Medicine, Guo Wei Ban Yi Zheng Han [2024] No. 150, April 30, 2024) | Yes | Attachment 1, indicator 4 "Emergency Grading and Triage Implementation Rate (EM-ME-04)" explanation: "Emergency pre-examination triage shall be accurately graded according to scientific standards; the currently widely used standard divides patients into four levels according to the severity of the condition: level I is critically ill patients, who need immediate treatment; level II is severely ill patients, for whom assessment and treatment are often carried out simultaneously; level III is acute patients, who need diagnosis and treatment within a short time; level IV is non-acute or sub-acute patients, who are seen in order in the general diagnosis and treatment area" |
| <http://wjw.beijing.gov.cn/zwgk_20040/ylws/201912/t20191216_1242338.html> (Beijing Municipal Health Commission, "Work Plan for Strengthening Emergency Pre-examination Triage and Grading in Beijing" and its attachment "Beijing Hospital Emergency Pre-examination Triage Grading Standards (Trial)", December 2019) | Yes | "Adhere to the principle of giving priority to critically and severely ill patients. According to the severity of the patient's condition, follow the principle from severe to mild and from rapidly changing condition to relatively stable, and reasonably arrange the order of patient visits." "Set up an emergency pre-examination triage area at a prominent position at the entrance of the emergency department." "According to the principle of 'three zones and four levels' … distinguish 'dying, critically ill, acute, and non-acute'." Attachment adult table: level 1 dying "a life threat or deterioration of the condition that is occurring or about to occur, requiring immediate active intervention", handling "immediately carry out assessment and treatment, and arrange the patient to enter the resuscitation room or rescue room" (red); level 2 critically ill "immediately monitor vital signs, receive treatment within 10 minutes, arrange the patient to enter the rescue room" (red); level 3 acute "give priority to diagnosis and treatment before level 4 patients, arrange the patient to wait in the general diagnosis and treatment area; if the waiting time is more than 30 minutes, reassessment is required" (yellow); level 4 non-acute "seen in order, unless the condition changes, the waiting time is relatively long; if the waiting time is more than 4 hours, reassessment may be carried out" (green). The manual assessment indicators for level 1 include "heart rate >180 beats/min or <40 beats/min", "systolic blood pressure <70 mmHg", "SpO2 <80% with rapid breathing", "shock", "definite myocardial infarction", "acute disturbance of consciousness/unresponsive or only responding to painful stimuli (GCS<9)" |

Rating A: the definition of the four-level triage comes from a current National Health Commission document, and the response times for each level come from Beijing's implementation standard, both verbatim-checkable. Approach "time" — what this item gains back is not being delayed by being queued behind others, not a quantifiable mortality reduction, so the mortality approach is not applied. Benefit magnitude "medium": there is no relative reduction figure to apply, so it is set as medium by judgment (moving severe cases into the rescue room within 10 minutes is a substantive difference, but this item only guarantees the order, not the outcome). Cost money=0 time=little willpower=no, composite "high".

Not obtained: the Ministry of Health's "Guidelines for the Construction and Management of Emergency Departments (Trial)" (Wei Yi Zheng Fa [2009] No. 50) is the source of "three zones and four levels", but nhc.gov.cn returns 412 to the local machine across the whole site (both Invoke-WebRequest and headless Chrome failed), and the official PDF of the industry standard WS/T 390—2012 "Standardized Process for Hospital Emergency Departments" is a scanned copy without a text layer, so pdftotext cannot extract text. Therefore these two are not included in the source column, and instead the 2024 quality control indicators (national level, verbatim-checkable) plus the Beijing plan (response times, verbatim-checkable) are cited. The State Council policy document library does not include the National Health Commission's departmental documents, so searching that library and finding nothing is normal.

Rating A: the original text of the standard is verbatim-checkable. Cost money=little time=medium willpower=some, cost score 3, composite "average".

The assessment fee "several hundred to two thousand yuan" is written in the cost column; it is the common range of judicial assessment fees in various places, and there is no nationally unified pricing document to cite, so it does not enter the benefit column and is not asserted as a figure; the cost column only writes the range and states that the applicant advances it first.

## Section 13, items 41 and 42 (penetrating injury foreign objects, fractures)

| URL | Rechecked | Original quoted passage |
|---|---|---|
| <https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFRC%20International%20First%20Aid,%20Resuscitation%20and%20Education%20Guidelines%202025.pdf> (International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies, "International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025", official PDF, with a text layer) | Yes | Pages 256–257: "First aid providers should apply pressure around an embedded object (e.g. a knife) to stabilize the object. Avoid removing the object." Pages 267–268: "Impaled objects in the body should be stabilized, and if the object is pulsating, it should be allowed to do so while loosely stabilized." "First aid providers should not push internal organs back into the body." "Do not remove any impaled objects from an abdominal or chest wound, as this may cause serious bleeding and make it more difficult for a medical professional to determine the severity of the wound." "An open chest wound that is not actively bleeding may be left open, without the application of a dressing, because a sealed wound may allow air to build up in the chest, which may lead to the lung partially or fully collapsing (pneumothorax)." "First aid providers should access emergency medical care for all penetrating chest or abdominal wounds." Pages 301–302: "Any injury to an extremity should be approached as a potential bone fracture." "The injury should be stabilized to minimize any movement and pain either manually (e.g. self-splinting) or mechanically (e.g. using a sling and bandages)." "first aid providers may ice or cool the sprained joint or soft tissue injury for up to 20 consecutive minutes, as this may decrease the pain. Cooling for longer than this may damage the skin." "If a long bone is injured, such as the femur, the bleeding from the bone itself can be life-threatening." "The only accurate way to diagnose a fracture is with an X-ray. When in doubt, assume it is a fracture." "Access emergency medical services (EMS) if the person has a lot of pain or swelling, signs of shock, the fracture is in the femur or if the limb is in an abnormal position." "When in a remote environment (or one with limited resources) and the limb below the angulated fracture is cool, pale or the first aid provider is unable to splint for movement, the first aid provider may consider straightening it if trained to do so." "If the injury is an open fracture, your priority is to stop the bleeding and limit infection." Evidence section: the only retrospective cohort on prehospital traction splinting of femoral fractures showed "a statistically significant decrease in units of blood transfusion needed within the first 24 hours and a statistically significant shorter length of hospital stay", but "a statistically significant decrease in mortality and complications could not be demonstrated, and the evidence was of very low certainty"; for reduction versus in-situ splinting, "no evidence was found" |

Both are set at B: the guidelines list these as good practice points, with no randomized trials behind them; the traction splinting item has only one very-low-certainty retrospective cohort, and there is simply no evidence on whether to reduce. Item 41 has benefit magnitude "large" — mortality approach, preventing fatal consequences such as immediate massive bleeding after pulling out a foreign object and tension pneumothorax; cost money=0 time=little willpower=some, cost score 1, composite "high". Item 42 has benefit magnitude "medium" — what it mainly prevents is secondary injury and disability, not death (the sentence about femoral fracture blood loss is an exception, but the main action of this item is stabilization and cold application); cost all zero, composite "high".

Not used: the NICE NG37 (complex fractures) page returned 403 to the local machine; the 2020 ILCOR International Consensus on First Aid Science (doi:10.1161/cir.0000000000000897) has no penetrating foreign objects in its abstract topics, only compression bandaging for closed joint injuries, so it is not cited. The Red Cross Society of China's first aid training materials have no publicly verbatim-checkable version, so they are not cited.
